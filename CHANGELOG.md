## [1.3.0] - Unreleased

### Added
- A DeepSeek Harness (DSH) plugin, `dsh-gravitas`, for DSH 0.2.x from 0.2.1-rc.2 (`engines.dsh` is `>=0.2.1-rc.2 <0.3.0-0`; DSH refuses plugins outside their declared range, so DSH 0.3 is not supported yet): `package.json` at the repository root and `dsh/`. It serves the same built files as the Antigravity installers (`dist/<lang>/gravitas`): the `gravitas` skill through the DSH skill registry, and the always-on rule as a global system-prompt section. Two agent presets replace Antigravity's verifier agents: `gravitas-inspector` (read, `glob`, `grep`, `web_fetch`, `web_search`; the file tool is mounted with writing and editing switched off) for seven roles, and `gravitas-checker` (adds `pwsh` or `bash`) for the checker. A separate Host tool, `gravitas_inspect`, starts children on those presets, so DeepSeek's shipped presets are not overridden. The skill gets a DSH platform note in each language, appended when it loads, that maps Antigravity's tool names to DSH's and says how to start an inspector; the rule gets a one-line tool note. Language follows the system locale (`zh*`, `ja*`, `ko*`, else English) or the row's `language` config; `alwaysOnRule: false` keeps only the skill. No dependencies and no install scripts. Install with `dsh plugin --profile web add github:Emily2040/gravitas#v1.3.0`.
- `dsh/test/plugin.test.js` (Node) and `tests/scripts/test_dsh_plugin.py`: the manifest and patch, that the inspector preset has no tool that writes, edits or runs commands, every language's skill, rule and note, that the platform notes name the same tools in every language, the skill staying under DSH's 8,192-character tool-result pruning threshold, and that `package.json` lists every runtime file.
- READMEs: a DeepSeek Harness section in all four languages, with example requests. The Chinese section has six examples, since most DSH users read Chinese.

### Changed
- The skill's `compatibility` line names DeepSeek Harness, in all four languages.
- `scripts/release_check.py` treats `dsh/` and `package.json` as shipped.
- The always-on rule, item 4, in all four languages: every number, date, deadline, fee, step or quoted wording the answer adds beyond what was asked is checked or cut, not hedged; arithmetic is redone a second way; a deadline is given at its safe end. Written from the four added claims that lost round one of the DSH A/B. Measured in `docs/dsh-ab/round-two`: on a new held-out set of ten requests Gravitas won 19 votes to plain DSH's 16 (5 ties), but with a slightly lower mean score and more wrong added claims (30 against 23 by a blind relabel of the graders' notes), and answers 5% shorter than plain with no more hedging. On the round-one prompts, two of the four named errors are gone in both runs and the other two came back in one of two runs each. Not a win.
- The always-on rule's Files section, in all four languages: the ban on reading outside the workspace now has an exception for the active skill's own files (its references, agents and scripts), which sit outside the workspace for an installed DSH plugin.

### Verified
- On Windows 11 with DSH 0.2.1-rc.2: the plugin installed into the `web` profile with `dsh plugin add`, and a boot of that profile with a probe plugin read back the skill catalog (the `gravitas` skill with its note), the assembled system prompt (the rule at order 10100, in English and in Chinese), the tool list (`gravitas_inspect` with both presets in its `agent_preset` choices) and the preset roster (both presets, no activation diagnostics). `dsh plugin remove` uninstalled it and removed it from the profile's bundles. Graded runs on DSH: two rounds of blind A/B on deepseek-flash; round two, with every result, is in `docs/dsh-ab/round-two`.

## [1.2.0] - 2026-10-20

### Added
- `15-harness-probing.md`, in all four languages: how to improve the harness (briefs, gates, checklists, tool rules, model mix, handoff files) while the work runs, adapted from Google Research's Harness² (Apache-2.0, arXiv 2610.14224). Propose two variants of one brief that differ in one dimension, probe both on the same item with the same seed, gates, screener and critic, and let an agent that ran neither compose the better brief with the evidence that decided. Lessons persist in `HARNESS_LESSONS.md` at the workspace root, one entry per probe with the evidence, the adopted rule and where it now lives; a lesson is retired with the evidence that retired it, never deleted. One probe pair per round by default, on the item that failed worst; guardrails against probing the gates themselves, against letting a probe agent judge its own pair, and against adopting on narration. The orchestrator's loading matrix names it for standard scale and above, before each round after the first, and `09-orchestration.md` points to it from staged escalation.

### Changed
- `13-platform.md`: the headless `401` row no longer says "about the hour mark". In 12 logged runs on 1.4.0 to 1.4.2 the cut came at 18, 28, 42, 53, 58, 61, 138, 208, 265 and 486 minutes, two runs of 60 and 383 minutes finished clean, and one turn ended instead with `NOT_FOUND (code 404): models/barium-b-agy is not found`; the files and subagent work survived every time. The orchestrator's report sentence says the same: a cut can come at any time, so every output and the report are written before the final reply.

## [1.1.0] - 2026-10-18

Changes drawn from an audit of real Antigravity conversations on this machine in October 2026: of 95 reasoning defects found across the audited runs, 37 came from 15 runs with this plugin loaded. The native support for Linux that people asked for is here too.

### Added
- `Install Gravitas (Linux).sh`, the Mac installer with the macOS parts replaced: the default language comes from `LANGUAGE`, `LC_ALL`, `LC_MESSAGES` or `LANG`, beta 1's hook is taken out of `hooks.json` by the first Python 3 that runs instead of JavaScript for Automation, there is no `xattr` step, and the messages name a file manager and a terminal. It is run from a terminal with `bash "Install Gravitas (Linux).sh"`, because a double-click on most Linux desktops opens a shell script in a text editor. Each README has an On Linux section. `tests/scripts/test_installers.py` runs it through every case the Mac installer gets, under Git Bash on Windows, and runs its Python on its own against what `install.py` writes; `release_check.py`, `build_release_zip.py`, `readme_parity.py` and `test_release.py` know the third installer. Like the Mac installer, it has not yet run on the system it is for.
- `scripts/narration_check.py`: lists every sentence in an answer that says a check was done ("I verified the transitions", "the figures were checked", "inspection of the sheet reveals") and names no command, file, capture, URL, label or exit status in the same sentence. The commonest defect in the audited runs was a check that never happened, written as a done thing; on the reasoning of one audited run it flags 7 such sentences, including the false claim that the sheets showed a jump cut every 24 frames. English only. `tests/scripts/test_narration_check.py` covers it in 14 cases, and the orchestrator runs it with `provenance_check.py` before the final reply.
- `01-evidence.md`: `observed` reaches only as far as the view you had. Frames sampled from a video show the sampled instants and nothing between them, so a label names the view with the artifact and writes what the view cannot show as not assessed. A section on narrated verification records the defect and the rule: a check is a tool event.
- `06-perceptual-gates.md`: a Sampled Views section, built from a run that compared two videos through contact sheets, labelled seamless transitions and no flicker `observed`, read the sampler's frame numbers as text overlays, and never measured the one thing the sheets could show. It gives the method: record the instrument, measure what the samples can show with a tool, write the rest as not assessed in the field that asks for it, and keep rendered, technically checked, perceptually reviewed, not assessed and blocked apart.
- `05-acceptance.md`: The Measure Is Not The Goal, from the largest group of defects in the audited runs with this plugin loaded: audio shaped hop by hop to pass an onset meter, grey fills added so frame differences grew monotonically, a grid drift kept to pass a frozen-time check, cells hidden to pass a text-clipping gate, render stills passed off as concept keyframes. When a gate and what it stands for pull apart, the thing wins, and a critic's perfect score does not outrank a defect already known.
- `gravitas-visual-critic` decides first which questions the image can answer at all, returns the rest in a new `not_assessed` field with the reason, repeats the instrument record in `instrument`, and treats a sampler's marks as not part of the image. The shared evidence block in every verifier says the same about sampled views and about claims of checks that name no tool step.

### Changed
- The always-on rule's floor, in all four languages: a check is a tool event, so a sentence that says something was verified names what ran or was opened, or goes; a sampled or summarised view shows what is in it and nothing beyond, and a required field takes that limit as its answer; a harness's status, a tool's summary line or an earlier sentence of the agent's own is not evidence that a check passed; a measure the person set is met as they meant it, never by reshaping the work to fit the number; a correction rewrites every reason it reaches; a step that has failed twice is diagnosed, not retried unchanged; and after the conversation has been summarised, what the reply relies on is re-read or re-run. Each line answers a defect pattern from the audit: narrated verification, misread sampled evidence, premature success on harness signals, metric gaming, perseveration after a correction, and errors that survived a context summary.
- `README.md` says that on Windows and a Mac no command is typed and on Linux one line is, and every README's limits say the Mac and Linux installers have not run on a Mac or on Linux.

### Known limits
- The new module sections in `01-evidence.md`, `05-acceptance.md` and `06-perceptual-gates.md`, and the new agent text, are English-only in this version; the zh-CN, ja and ko modules are unchanged. The rule's new floor lines are in all four languages.
- Neither the new rule lines nor `narration_check.py` has been benchmarked. They were written from the audit, not measured against it.

## [1.0.0] - 2026-10-11

The first official release of Gravitas, after two private betas. Everything below changed since 1.0.0-beta.2.

### Added
- New READMEs in English, 简体中文, 日本語 and 한국어, each written for its own readers rather than translated. Each opens with one sentence on what Gravitas does and the double-click install; Python and the terminal appear only in the technical part. Each shows real examples from requests in its own language: the question, short excerpts of the answer without and with Gravitas, why the difference matters, the full answers, and one case where Gravitas did worse. A shared section shows examples from the other languages tested, in their own words with a gloss. Then come a plain answer to why Gravitas, when thousands of skills exist, the results with their vote counts, the method, the architecture and the limits. The examples and the older numbers were measured on the previous rule (v4), and every README says so.
- `docs/examples/`: the request, both answers in full and verbatim, the graders' verdicts and what happened, for every example the READMEs show: four in English, four in Japanese, three in Korean and four in Simplified Chinese, each set with one case where Gravitas did worse, and one file each for Arabic, Bengali, Spanish, French, Hindi, Indonesian, Portuguese and Russian. The Bengali and Indonesian files say why no case was clear enough to use: plain Antigravity also got the key point right.
- `docs/comparison.md`: the evidence behind the comparison, from research on 24 September 2026. It holds fifteen features, 18 projects read in depth with the evidence for every cell, the four novelty claims and the refuters that narrowed three of them, and all 63 candidates found.
- `plugin/README.md` ships inside the installed plugin, and a translated install writes the translated README and plugin description, the text Antigravity lists for the plugin. `check_skills.py --repo` fails when a translation lacks either.
- 173 tests for the four scripts in `tests/scripts/`, runnable with plain Python.
- `scripts/readme_parity.py` checks that every README gives the same facts in its own words. The native READMEs need not follow the English structure, so for each one, and for each locale's plugin README, it compares with the English README the installer file names, the commands in code blocks, and the win shares and vote counts. In every README it checks that each relative link and heading link resolves, as GitHub spells them, and that the language-switch line is there. `tests/scripts/test_readme_parity.py` covers it in 25 cases.
- `14-checks.md` and six checks in `skills/gravitas/scripts/checks/` for forms a model miscounts while sounding sure: `pingze_check.py` (绝句 and 律诗, by 平水韵 or 中华新韵), `couplet_check.py` (对联), `mora_count.py` (haiku, tanka and senryu, from a kana reading), `sijo_check.py` (평시조), `num_units.py` (amounts across 万, 亿, 億, 兆, 만, 억, 조, lakh, crore and million, and 人民币大写 and Korean 공문 amounts) and `locale_dates.py` (dates for zh-CN, ja and ko readers, and 和暦). They need only Python 3.8, print `PASS`, `FAIL` or `ERROR` on the first line and exit 0, 1 or 2. The two verse checks share `zh_tones.py` and two data files that record their sources and licences: the 106 平水韵 groups from zh.wikisource and Mandarin readings from pypinyin 0.55.0. `scripts/build_check_data.py` rebuilds the data and is not shipped. The orchestrator loads the module when the work holds one of these forms, in all four languages. A check runs only through `run_command`; where that is refused, the module says to apply the same rules by hand and record that the script did not run. `tests/scripts/test_checks.py` sees each check fail on a known bad sample and pass on a known good one.
- A Second Opinion section in the always-on rule. For an answer the person will rely on, act on or send, the agent writes the whole draft, starts `gravitas-reviewer` with `Model: "pro"` on the request and the draft, waits with a 180-second `schedule` timer set to `TimerCondition: "any"` while its turn ends on one holding line, and then applies only findings that show, with evidence, an error in what the person will rely on or a gap the rule would fill, by correcting or deleting and adding at most one sentence per finding. It is skipped whenever waiting costs more than a check can gain: anything that cannot wait, a person who came to be met, each turn of a live scene or chat, a one-line answer or a greeting. It is off by default: in testing with the previous rule it did not beat the rule alone, and it roughly doubles the time an answer takes. Install with `--review` to use it.
- `gravitas-reviewer`, a ninth agent. It returns at most three defects as JSON, checked in a fixed order from wrong facts to wrong format, and never rewrites the draft. It holds `run_command` for arithmetic, so its read-only status is a charter rule. `build_agents.py` generates it without the shared evidence block.
- `install.py --no-review`, passed through by `install.sh` and `install.ps1`, installs the rule without the Second Opinion section and leaves out `gravitas-reviewer`. `--review` forces it on. Without either, the constant `REVIEW_DEFAULT` decides; it is `False` in this release. The zh-CN, ja and ko rules carry the same section. A rule without the marked section installs without `gravitas-reviewer`, and `--review` then stops the install.
- `TESTS.md` check A13: a reviewed answer arrives once, with at most one holding line.
- `Install Gravitas (Windows).cmd` and `Install Gravitas (Mac).command` at the top of the repository install Gravitas with a double-click, with no Python, terminal or `agy`. Each says in one line what it will do, offers English, 简体中文, 日本語 and 한국어 with the default taken from the system's display language, and offers Remove Gravitas; their messages are written for each of the four languages. They copy `dist/<lang>/gravitas` into a dated backup folder and check it there, then move any earlier copy and a beta 1 skill into that folder and move the new copy into place, putting back whatever was moved if a step fails. They take beta 1's `gravitas-always-on` hook out of `hooks.json` as `install.py` does (PowerShell on Windows, JavaScript for Automation on a Mac), keep a copy, and say so; where they cannot, they leave the hook and say where it is. After installing they say how to quit Antigravity from its icon and open it again. Remove also removes a beta 1 install. `install.py` stays the route with options such as `--review`.
- `dist/`: the plugin folder for each language, ready to copy. `scripts/build_dist.py` writes it with `install.py`'s own code, so each folder is exactly what `install.py <lang>` installs with no options. It validates every language before writing and swaps the new `dist/` in only when all of them pass. `tests/scripts/test_build_dist.py` fails when `dist/` in the checkout differs from a fresh build, and `tests/scripts/test_installers.py` runs both installers in throwaway home folders, including with files held open, a folder that cannot be written and two runs at once, and compares the result with `install.py`'s, byte for byte.
- `scripts/release_check.py`, to run before a release: it fails when anything a download ships is uncommitted, when the committed `dist/` is not a build of the committed sources, when an installer has the wrong line endings or mode, or when a README links a download of another version. `scripts/build_release_zip.py` makes `Gravitas.zip` from a commit, holding only the two installers, `LICENSE` and `dist/`, for attaching to a release. `tests/scripts/test_release.py` covers both without writing to the repository.

### Fixed
- `provenance_check.py` pairs every tool call with its own result, so a failed fetch or a denied command no longer counts as opened or run; checks a quote against the source the label cites; matches URLs and paths exactly, with a parent page as a warning; compares the whole command, keeping case and argument spacing; reads `steps/<n>/output.txt`; recognises POSIX paths; and survives malformed rows, CJK quotes and Windows pipes. Its verdicts on the seven real test answers are unchanged.
- `ledger.py` reports never-voted findings at quick scale as unverified; records blocked and demoted votes without counting them; holds votes to the same provenance rule as findings; requires a `read` source to quote content, not a path; flags nearby duplicates instead of silently dropping them unless the wording is near-identical; validates records against the schemas; and handles blocked finder envelopes and files with a byte order mark.
- `slopcheck.py` gains `--strict`, which exits 1 on any flag; matches banned words on word boundaries while still catching derived forms; flags any em-dash, conclusion crutches and an unclosed code fence; skips quoted, blockquoted and fenced questions; and survives Windows pipes and missing files.
- `check_skills.py` bars file-writing tools from every verifier role, not only critics, and reports malformed tool fields instead of crashing. The refuter's verdict schema gains `status` and `error`.
- Module corrections in all four languages: the chocolate-factory suite had 8 gates, not 9; the automated-accessibility figure now cites the 2017 UK government tool audit and Deque's 2021 study; `restart_dev_server` is marked as unconfirmed; the triage module records the retest that fixed the control case; the platform module records that the `--json-schema` defect was fixed in agy 1.2.14 (#1064). `TESTS.md` gives the Gaussian integral as 0.886227, and the English `SKILL.md` compatibility line names the agy versions it was tested on, 1.2.9 to 1.2.16.
- `install.py` reads a legacy `hooks.json` that starts with a byte order mark, and reports one that does not parse and carries on, instead of stopping with a traceback; gives each run its own backup folder, so two runs in the same second no longer overwrite a backup; prints home folder names the console code page cannot encode; and exits 1 when the installed plugin fails its check, a check that now covers this plugin alone. `install.sh` uses the first Python that runs, so the Microsoft Store stub for `python3` no longer stops it.
- `install.py` no longer leaves a `__pycache__` folder inside the installed plugin when it loads the plugin's `check_skills.py` to validate it.
- `install.py` builds and validates the new plugin inside the backup folder before touching the installed one, keeps the plugin it replaces in the backup instead of deleting it, moves folders by renaming them so a file held open cannot leave a half-moved folder, and leaves editor and file-manager leftovers (`.DS_Store`, `Thumbs.db`, `*~` and the like) out of every install and of `dist/`. `--uninstall` also removes a beta 1 skill and hook.

### Changed
- The READMEs, in all four languages, report the benchmark of the previous rule (v4): blind pairwise grading against plain Antigravity with Google's ten plugins installed, against `/boost` and against the earlier rule-only version, with votes, intervals and cost per model, and a portable core of v4 run in Codex and Grok on eight models and graded by GPT-6 Sol. They report where v4 did not win as plainly as the wins: the edge set (history, deep knowledge, wordplay and creative writing) and some models, such as Codex Astra and Grok 4.5.
- The plugin description is written for people choosing a plugin rather than for engineers.
- The always-on rule now governs answers given in the conversation instead of telling the agent when to load the skill. It was rebuilt from a study of v4's 118 clear losses to plain Antigravity or `/boost` in the benchmark, the comparisons where at least two graders preferred the other answer and at least half of all graders called the margin clear. 109 were real losses, and they fall into ten mechanisms. In 78 a v4 line pushed the model into the loss, most often a line with a fixed shape: one that set a position, fired a section on a topic or cut by the form of a sentence, whatever its effect on the person. In 28 v4 had no line that would have caught the failure, and 3 were faults in the test harness. v4 checked the premise but never tested the answer as the person would use it. The new rule reads the person's words as meant, in the frame they set, and sizes every part of the answer, warnings and doubts included, by what it does for what they came for. Seven lines say what the best answer does: it gives what the best person the message calls on would give; leads with what they need first; tests what they rely on as they will use it; states each claim as exactly and surely as the evidence allows; judges meaning by the frame and action by the world, correcting kindly and only as far as the error reaches; writes each piece for its reader; and writes to its practice's standard, taking technical text about 80% of the way to ASD-STE100 or its language's own plain technical style. A floor, never worse than plain, keeps everything a strong plain answer gives and says how to answer someone who may be in danger. The rule went through four drafts, each attacked by an adversary with requests built to break it. It passed a release check from a real install: 17 cases, with chat and role play fine in all four languages and, after two fixes to the floor, the crisis case in each language judged better than plain Antigravity on the same model. It has not yet had the full benchmark. The `gravitas` skill is now for work one pass cannot check: audits, reviews, changes across many files, research reports and builds.
- The zh-CN, ja and ko rules, and the changed modules in all three languages, are written for native readers from the English rule's content. Where the English rule names ASD-STE100 for technical writing, each names its own language's plain-style standard: 阮一峰《中文技术文档的写作规范》 with GB/T 15834-2011 and GB/T 15835-2011 for zh-CN, 文化審議会「公用文作成の考え方」(2022) for ja, and for ko the 한글 맞춤법, with the principles of 쉬운 우리말 written out in the rule itself. The benchmark ran the English rule in every language, so the native rules have not been benchmarked yet.
- `SKILL.md` and the orchestrator send questions, advice and short writing to the rule, and the orchestrator looks inside the workspace only when the request names files, a folder or a project. Deep work writes its report, with tiers and coverage, to a file, and the reply gives the verdict and findings in plain words, as the rule requires.
- `07-subagents.md`, `08-delegation.md` and `13-platform.md` record how to wait for a subagent, verified headless with `--sandbox --dangerously-skip-permissions` on agy 1.2.16 on 3 October 2026; at default settings, which refuse `run_command` and `read_url_content`, it is untested. `schedule` returns at once and a pending one-shot timer keeps the run alive. A subagent that finishes without calling `send_message` wakes the parent with a message from sender `system`, so a timer keyed to the subagent's ID is not cancelled by it: the timer ran its full 240 seconds in 7 of 7 runs and the parent replied twice, while `TimerCondition: "any"` was cancelled in 8 of 8. On Gemini 3.8 Flash a turn with neither text nor tool calls was rejected, so the waiting turn says one line, which a headless run prints. Without a timer the result is still lost, as on 1.2.9.
- The plugin ships no hooks. A hook layer that injected reminder cards on keywords and checked each draft before sending was tested against the rule alone and left out. Of the 21 cases where the rule alone beat the plain answer and the rule with hooks did not, the reminder cards caused 8; the draft check left the reply unchanged in 9 of the 21, at about twice the output tokens and 10 to 25 seconds more per turn; and it was skipped on about a third of turns. The installer moves a `hooks.json` left in the plugin folder by a development build, and that build's log folder `~/.gemini/gravitas`, into its timestamped backup, on install and on uninstall.
- `TESTS.md` grades the twelve prompts on what the rule asks for (premise checked, independent check, proxy refused, decisive caveat) instead of tier labels; A3 fails when a question opens the skill or an audit does not; A1 and A4 count eight agents, or nine with `--review`.
- Version 1.0.0 in `plugin.json` and `SKILL.md`.
- Backups go to `~/.gemini/gravitas-backups/<date-time>`, shared by `install.py` and the double-click installers, instead of `~/.gemini/config/_backup_gravitas_<date-time>`. A beta 1 skill is saved there as `skills-gravitas`. On Windows without PowerShell the folder is named `backup` instead of the date and time.
- The design had a second outside review and the repository a second proofread, and every claim was checked against the files before anything changed. Of the review's 132 design claims, 43 held up, and they led to these changes. The reply to someone who may be in danger is now sized to what they said: a warning sign alone is named gently, with somewhere to talk, and the safety question and crisis lines come when harm or thoughts of ending their life are shown. Speech level and form of address follow the reader of each piece, the reply included. Three rule clauses are clearer: when to test, when to make a pick the person hands over, and when to list a folder the request names. In the checks, digits after 零 keep their written place, a spoken amount such as 3억 5천 is read as said and flagged, ranges joined by 至 or 到 are checked, more Korean and Japanese date errors are caught, a couplet fails on 同位重字, and a `PASS` names any character the tables could not judge. Of the proofread's 178 findings, 87 were fixed.

### Known limits
- In Japanese and Korean, the reply to someone who may be in danger can answer a person who wrote casually in a polite register, although the rule asks for the person's own register. Plain Antigravity does the same.
- The native zh-CN, ja and ko rules have not been benchmarked. The benchmark used the English rule in every language.
