## The request

````text
Good evening. I'm the Secretary of our Residents Association here in Lokogoma, Abuja, and I've been asked to give the vote of thanks this Saturday when we commission the new borehole and the solar street lights. Money came from residents' levy plus a big donation from one of our landlords, Alhaji Kabiru (he doesn't like too much praise o). The Area Council chairman is sending a representative. I also want to thank the project committee (Engr. Tunde chaired it), the contractor, and our vigilante boys who have been guarding the panels at night. Maybe 2 to 3 minutes. Make it respectful but add small humour, people are tired of long speeches.
````

### **Quick Delivery Tips for Saturday**

1. **Alhaji Kabiru’s mention:** When saying *"Alhaji, please close your ears for ten seconds"*, look warmly in his direction with a polite nod/smile. It brings pleasant laughter from the crowd while respecting his aversion to loud flattery.
2. **The Vigilante Boys:** When thanking them, point or gesture towards where they are standing so the crowd turns to clap directly for them. It builds strong morale.
3. **The Area Council Representative:** The light joke about the access road is a classic, respectful Nigerian community touch — it puts a gentle smile on the representative’s face without being confrontational.
4. **Copy Saved:** A copy has also been saved to your workspace as [vote_of_thanks_speech.md](file:///…/vote_of_thanks_speech.md) so you can print it or read it directly from your phone.

## Gravitas's answer

````text
The Representative of the Executive Chairman of the Area Council,

Elders, landlords, neighbours, and distinguished guests,  

Good afternoon.

Protocol having been established, I am very mindful of the golden rule of giving a vote of thanks: be brief, be sincere, and never stand between hungry people and their refreshments. So I promise not to keep us.

First, on behalf of our entire residents’ association, I want to express our profound gratitude to the Executive Chairman of our Area Council, ably represented here today by [Name/Title of Representative]. Thank you for taking out time from your busy schedule to witness our community’s self-help initiative. Your presence shows that our local government recognizes grassroots efforts, and we look forward to even greater collaboration with the Council.

To our wonderful residents and landlords: congratulations to all of us. This is our collective victory. In this current economy where every single naira is fighting for its life, you paid your levies. Even when we disturbed your peace with payment reminders morning, afternoon, and night on the estate WhatsApp group, you still answered the call. Thank you for your patience and your sacrifice.

Now, to our respected elder and landlord, Alhaji Kabiru. Alhaji strictly warned me earlier today that he does not want noise or public praise. So Alhaji, please close your ears for just ten seconds: thank you immensely for your extraordinary generosity. When this project needed that major push to cross the finish line, you stepped in quietly and heavily. May the Almighty replenish your pockets in manifold ways, grant you good health, and bless your household.

A huge thank you goes to our Project Committee, led by the tireless Engr. Tunde. Thank you for the sleepless nights, the technical supervision under the hot Lokogoma sun, and for ensuring we drilled deep enough to get clean, running water. To our contractor and the technical crew, thank you for delivering quality work on schedule and ensuring our streets are bright and our taps are flowing.

Finally, we cannot leave here without appreciating our dedicated vigilante team. While the rest of us were sleeping soundly in our beds, these men were out in the dark, keeping an eagle eye on our solar panels, batteries, and cables. If not for your vigilance, we might be telling a different story today. We see your sacrifices, and we thank you.

As we celebrate, let us remember that the project has ended, but maintenance has just begun. These facilities belong to all of us; let us guard them, use the water responsibly, and treat this infrastructure like our personal property.

Thank you very much, enjoy the rest of the afternoon, and may God continue to bless our estate.
````

## How the graders voted

The answer key asked for a Nigerian-style protocol opening with the Area Council chairman's representative first, thanks for every group the secretary named, a brief, warm thank-you to Alhaji Kabiru that respects his dislike of praise, light local humour, about two to three minutes, and a warm send-off. It ruled out gushing over the donor, invented details such as the amount donated or the representative's name, and American idioms.

- Gemini 3.1 Pro preferred Gravitas, by a decisive margin: Gravitas had the Nigerian protocol opening, and plain Antigravity a generic greeting.
- Gemini 3.8 Flash preferred Gravitas, by a clear margin: plain Antigravity left the representative out of its opening, and Gravitas put a placeholder in brackets where the representative's name goes.

Both graders also marked plain Antigravity down for saying it had saved a copy of the speech to a file. The run record shows it did use its file-writing tool in that run, so I don't hold that against it.

The same request also ran on Gemini 3.1 Pro, and both graders preferred Gravitas there too, by clear margins: 4 of 4 Gemini votes across the two models. On Pro, plain Antigravity did observe protocol, in the line after its greeting ("Protocols duly observed, starting from the esteemed representative of our Area Council Chairman"), so the difference there was register: both graders pointed to its "the real MVPs" as slang that did not belong in the speech.

GPT-6 Sol graded both runs later. On Flash it also preferred Gravitas, by a clear margin, and it too said plain Antigravity placed the representative too late in its opening. On Pro it preferred plain Antigravity, by a clear margin: it found that Gravitas's jokes there, about the chairman owing the estate a project, people borrowing from the donor and chasing the contractor, could make the occasion awkward. With its votes counted, Gravitas got 5 of 6.

## What happened

### What plain Antigravity missed

In Nigeria a vote of thanks, like most speeches at a public function, opens with protocol: the speaker greets the important guests by title, the most senior first, before saying anything else. At this event the most senior guest is the representative the Area Council chairman is sending. Plain Antigravity opened with "Good afternoon, distinguished guests, elders, fellow landlords, and neighbors." The representative is missing from that greeting. The speech did thank the council first once it got going, but the greeting is where protocol is observed, and leaving out the council's representative there can read as a slight.

It also wrapped the speech in a preface, three delivery tips and a note about the saved file, so the secretary would have to cut the speech out of the answer before using it.

### What Gravitas did

Its answer was the speech and nothing else. It opened with the representative, then the estate chairman and executives, then elders, landlords, neighbours and guests, followed by "Protocol having been established". The representative's name went in brackets, since the request didn't give it. It thanks everyone the secretary named. The line to Alhaji Kabiru is a short joke about his modesty followed by a blessing. The humour is local: residents who paid their levies "Even when we disturbed your peace with payment reminders morning, afternoon, and night on the estate WhatsApp group", in an economy "where every single naira is fighting for its life". It spells "neighbours" the British way, as Nigerian English does.

Plain Antigravity's jokes were local too (the committee watched the drilling "like WAEC exam supervisors"), and both speeches run to about 450 words, roughly three minutes or a little more when spoken. Neither the humour nor the length was the difference.

### Which part of Gravitas did it

Move 1, recognising the practice: this is a Nigerian vote of thanks, and that practice has a set opening. Move 3, fitting the person: their English, and the piece itself, up front and on its own. The v4 rule says nothing about protocol; the model already knew the convention. What the rule did was point it at the person's own language and at the piece they asked for:

> Answer first, in plain words and in the person's language.

> A piece of writing comes first, in one version unless they asked for options, and within any length they set (count before you finish).

> Use the shape and medium they asked for: an email ready to paste has no Markdown or LaTeX in it, and a detail they did not give and you cannot infer goes in [brackets].

### Why it matters to the person asking

The secretary speaks for the whole estate, in front of a government representative. Gravitas's version can be read out as written, with one name to fill in, and it greets the room the way the room expects.
