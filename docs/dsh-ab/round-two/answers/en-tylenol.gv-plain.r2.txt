I’m glad you’re looking out for her, but I can’t write that plan as-is—the Tylenol dose is unsafe, and NyQuil would compound it.

**Why:** 2 × 500 mg every 4 hours = **6,000 mg/day**. The Extra Strength label max is usually **6 caplets (3,000 mg) in 24 hours**, and even the most permissive adult ceiling is **4,000 mg/day**. At 78, her weight, liver/kidney health, alcohol use, and other medicines matter. **NyQuil contains acetaminophen**, so it can double-dose her, and its sedating antihistamine raises confusion and fall risk.

**Send this:**

> Hi [Name] — Mom had a tooth out yesterday. Please **don’t** start the 2 Extra Strength Tylenol every 4 hours plan — that’s 6,000 mg/day, way over the safe max. And **don’t give NyQuil at all tonight** — it contains acetaminophen, so it can double-dose her, and the sedating antihistamine raises her confusion/fall risk.
>
> Before the next dose, call the dentist or pharmacist and ask: “She’s 78, had an extraction — what’s the exact acetaminophen dose and daily maximum, and what’s safe for her cold and sleep?” Give only what they confirm.
>
> If they confirm the label dose of **2 caplets (500 mg each)** and say her weight/liver/kidneys are okay: **8 am, 2 pm, 8 pm** — 6 caplets/3,000 mg in 24 hours, and **no middle-of-the-night dose** unless they give a different max.
>
> If they confirm **1 caplet**: **8 am, 2 pm, 8 pm, and 2 am only if she’s awake and in pain** — don’t wake her just to dose. Never double up for a missed dose.
>
> Write down the time and amount of every dose. Don’t add ibuprofen, aspirin, Benadryl, or any cold/nighttime product without asking the pharmacist. If she’s already had Tylenol or NyQuil today, total up the acetaminophen and tell the pharmacist—or call Poison Control if it’s over the max. If she seems confused, unusually sleepy, has stomach pain, or vomits, call me and Poison Control (1-800-222-1222 in the U.S.). Text me what the dentist says. Thanks.

**Also confirm with the dentist/pharmacist:** her weight, liver/kidney history, alcohol use, and other medicines—especially warfarin. For the cold, saline spray and fluids are safer than combination cold medicines. If her pain is worsening or she has swelling or fever, call the dentist.
