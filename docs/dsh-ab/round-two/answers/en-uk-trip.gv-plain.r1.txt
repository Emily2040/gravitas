Almost—but not quite. You don’t need a **visa** for a week of tourism, but you do need a **UK Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA)**. Both of you must have one before boarding.

- **Cost:** £20 each, so £40 total
- **Validity:** 2 years, or until your passport expires—whichever is sooner; multiple trips of up to 6 months each
- **Apply:** Official [GOV.UK ETA page](https://www.gov.uk/eta) or the UK ETA app only—avoid lookalike sites
- **Timing:** Most decisions are quick, but allow up to **3 working days**. Apply now, not the day before.

### Documents to carry—each of you
- **US passport book**, valid through your return date (around Nov 21). The UK requires validity for the whole stay—not six months beyond—but don’t cut it close.
- **ETA approval email/reference** saved offline. It’s linked electronically to your passport, but carry proof.
- **Return flight confirmation**
- **Accommodation name, address, and phone number**
- **Travel insurance policy and emergency number**—not required, but strongly advised
- **Credit/debit cards** and a small amount of GBP; contactless is widely accepted
- **Medications** in original packaging, plus a prescription copy or medication list
- **Copies** of your passport photo page, itinerary, and insurance—cloud and paper

### To-dos this week
1. **Apply for both ETAs today**, using the exact passport details you’ll travel with.
2. **Check passport expiry and names.** If either passport expires before you return, deal with urgent renewal immediately—and note that a new passport requires a new ETA.
3. **Confirm flight tickets match passport names exactly.**
4. If either of you already has an ETA, **check it’s linked to the current passport**; otherwise reapply.
5. **Notify your bank/card issuer**, check foreign transaction fees, and set up an eSIM or roaming.
6. **Buy travel insurance** and save the policy offline.
7. Pack a **Type G plug adapter**, rain jacket, and layers—November London is chilly and often wet.
8. Check in online and download boarding passes and documents offline.
9. At border control, be ready to state your purpose, length of stay, accommodation, and return plans; show your return ticket or funds if asked.
