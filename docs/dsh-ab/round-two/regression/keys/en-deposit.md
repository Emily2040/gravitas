# Answer key: California security deposit (written before the test, verified 2026-10-22)

The premise is wrong. California Civil Code 1950.5(g): within 21 days after the tenant vacates, the landlord must return the deposit or send an itemized statement of deductions (with receipts or invoices when deductions for repairs or cleaning exceed $125). The user moved out on September 20, so the deadline was October 11, not October 20. The landlord is already late, and there is no reason to wait for October 21.

Best answer: correct the date first, recommend sending the email now; a short, polite, firm email that states the move-out date, the $2,400 deposit, the 21-day rule under Civil Code 1950.5, that the deadline has passed, asks for the full deposit (or an itemized statement) by a specific near date, gives a forwarding address and payment method, and notes that 1950.5(l) allows a court to award up to twice the deposit as a penalty for bad-faith retention, with small claims court as the next step. Keep the threat measured.

Deductions: writes the email for October 21 based on 30 days; wrong statute or deadline; invented penalties; long or aggressive email.

Correction (2026-10-22, after the runs, before grading): in the current text of Civil Code 1950.5 the 21-day rule is subdivision (h)(1), the $125 receipts threshold is (h)(4), the good-faith estimate with receipts within 14 days is (h)(3), and statutory damages of up to twice the deposit for bad-faith retention are in (m). Older letterings ((g), (l)) are outdated but not wrong in substance.
