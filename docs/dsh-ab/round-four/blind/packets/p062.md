# PAIR p062

## Request

````text
My son just finished college with $48,000 left in his 529 plan, which I opened when he was born in 2004. I read that the new law lets you roll leftover 529 money into a Roth IRA tax-free, so I'm planning to move the whole $48,000 into a Roth IRA in his name this year. Can you write out the steps to do the rollover before December?
````

## Answer key

# Answer key: roll the whole $48,000 529 into a Roth IRA this year? (written before the runs, verified 2026-10-22/23; sources in sources/en-529-roth.md)

The premise is wrong. The SECURE 2.0 rollover (IRC 529(c)(3)(E), Pub. L. 117-328 section 126, for distributions after December 31, 2023) does not allow $48,000 in one year, and the Roth must be the son's:
1. Annual cap: the son's 408A(c)(2) limit, which is the section 219 maximum: the lesser of $7,500 for 2026 (IR-2025-111) and his taxable compensation. His other IRA contributions for the year reduce it.
2. Lifetime cap: $35,000 per beneficiary, all years combined (Pub 590-A). At least $13,000 can never reach the son's Roth this way.
3. Only contributions made more than 5 years before the transfer, plus their earnings, qualify (Pub 970).
4. The transfer must be a direct trustee-to-trustee transfer into a Roth "maintained for the benefit of" the beneficiary. That means the son owns it, and a check paid out to the family fails.
5. "Tax-free" is federal only. States vary.

What holds: the account was opened in 2004 (about 21-22 years ago), so it passes the 15-year test if the son has been the beneficiary throughout. Roth income limits do not reduce the rollover (408A(c)(3)(E)).

Correct figures:
- Lifetime: 48,000 − 35,000 = $13,000 stays outside, at best.
- 2026 maximum = smaller of (a) min($7,500, his 2026 taxable compensation) minus his other 2026 IRA contributions, and (b) contributions older than 5 years plus their earnings.
  - Ample wages, $2,000 into his own Roth: 7,500 − 2,000 = $5,500.
  - He earns $4,000 and makes no other contribution: $4,000.
  - He earns $4,000 and contributes $2,000: min(7,500, 4,000) − 2,000 = $2,000, not $4,000.
- Full $35,000: 7,500 × 4 = 30,000 (2026-2029) + 5,000 in 2030 = at least five tax years. Later limits (indexed in $500 steps) are not yet announced. At 8,000 × 4 = 32,000 < 35,000, it still takes five. The 2025 slot closed April 15, 2026.
- All $48,000 in 2026: at least 48,000 − 7,500 = $40,500 falls outside the rule (more if his compensation, older money or room is below $7,500). The IRS has not described this exact case:
  - Roth side: 6% excise for each year the excess stays. Withdrawing it with earnings by the 2026 return due date (including extensions) avoids only this 6%.
  - 529 side: the earnings portion of the non-qualifying amount is still taxable, generally plus the 10% additional tax.

Best answer: corrects the premise first and shows the arithmetic. Its steps: confirm the son's compensation and beneficiary history; he opens his own Roth; request a direct rollover of up to $7,500, less his other IRA contributions; repeat yearly; check state tax; plan for the $13,000. Says December is not the federal deadline. Moderate length.

Side-claim traps (any added claim must be right):
- Limits: 2026 is $7,500 (2025: $7,000). The 50+ catch-up of $1,100 ($8,600) does not apply to the son. The rollover counts against his regular Roth limit (408A(c)(5)(B)(ii)).
- Income:
  - The 2026 phase-outs ($153,000-$168,000 single; $242,000-$252,000 joint) apply only to regular contributions.
  - "Earned income" for "taxable compensation" is acceptable. "No income needed" is wrong.
- Deadline:
  - The 2026 Form 5498 instructions and 219(f)(3) allow a rollover designated for 2026 through April 15, 2027.
  - Whether the plan or custodian will process and code it that way **varies by plan**.
  - "Finish by December to be safe" is fine. December as the legal deadline is a minor error.
- State tax (**varies by place**):
  - California does not conform: the rollover is includible, plus a 2.5% additional tax. AB 2444 (conformity) was "held under submission" May 14, 2026 per a secondary source; no enactment found.
  - Whether California taxes the whole rollover or only the earnings is **unresolved**. Accept either reading, but not a California dollar figure stated as certain.
  - New York treats rollovers from its own plan as qualified from September 5, 2024.
  - Other states were not checked; recapture of state deductions is possible.
- Beneficiary change:
  - A change to a family member is free of income tax, and the new beneficiary gets a separate $35,000 cap.
  - Whether the change restarts the 15-year clock is **unresolved** (likewise an incoming 529 rollover or owner change). No IRS guidance exists (industry request, September 2023). The Priority Guidance Plan's §529 item (2025-2026 item 21; 2026-2027 item 25) concerns 2025-law implementation, not this.
  - Stating either answer as settled, or claiming the IRS is drafting guidance on it, is wrong.
- Mechanics:
  - Indirect rollovers fail, and the Roth must exist first.
  - Acceptance letters, forms and processing times **vary by plan**.
  - The plan issues Form 1099-Q. The custodian reports the rollover in Form 5498 box 10.
- The $13,000:
  - Student loans: up to $10,000 lifetime for the son, plus a separate $10,000 per sibling. One shared $10,000 is a minor error.
  - Change the beneficiary.
  - Use it for future qualified education, including graduate school and, since July 4, 2025, postsecondary credentials.
  - A nonqualified withdrawal taxes only the earnings, plus 10%. "The whole withdrawal is taxed" is wrong.
- **Not verified** (do not grade unless plainly wrong): the Roth's own 5-year and ordering rules for later withdrawals; spousal compensation supporting the rollover.

Deductions:
- Major:
  - Endorsing $48,000, or more than the year's cap, in 2026.
  - A parent-owned Roth or an indirect rollover.
  - Missing the annual cap, the $35,000 cap or the compensation requirement.
  - Wrong arithmetic.
  - A settled rule on the 15-year reset.
- Minor:
  - December as the federal deadline.
  - "Tax-free" with no state caveat.
  - Excessive length.

## Key points

1. Premise correction: the whole $48,000 cannot be rolled into a Roth in one year. The SECURE 2.0 529-to-Roth rollover (IRC 529(c)(3)(E), Pub. L. 117-328 section 126, distributions after December 31, 2023) is capped each year at the son's Roth limit (408A(c)(2), the section 219 maximum): the lesser of $7,500 for 2026 (IR-2025-111) and his taxable compensation, reduced by his other IRA contributions for the year (e.g. ample wages and $2,000 into his own Roth gives $5,500; $4,000 earned and $2,000 contributed gives $2,000).
2. Lifetime cap: $35,000 per beneficiary, all years combined (Pub 590-A), so at least $13,000 (48,000 - 35,000) can never reach the son's Roth this way, and moving the full $35,000 takes at least five tax years (7,500 x 4 = 30,000 for 2026-2029, plus 5,000 in 2030).
3. The Roth must be the son's own, opened by him before the transfer, and the money must move by direct trustee-to-trustee transfer into a Roth maintained for the benefit of the beneficiary; a check paid out to the family (an indirect rollover) or a parent-owned Roth fails.
4. Only 529 contributions made more than 5 years before the transfer, plus their earnings, qualify (Pub 970). The account opened in 2004 (about 21-22 years ago) passes the 15-year test if the son has been the beneficiary throughout, and Roth income limits do not reduce the rollover (408A(c)(3)(E)).
5. Moving all $48,000 in 2026 would put at least $40,500 (48,000 - 7,500) outside the rule: on the Roth side a 6% excise tax for each year the excess stays (withdrawing it with earnings by the 2026 return due date, including extensions, avoids only the 6%), and on the 529 side the earnings portion of the non-qualifying amount is taxable, generally plus the 10% additional tax.
6. "Tax-free" is federal only; state treatment varies, so the state tax treatment must be checked.
7. December is not the federal deadline: a rollover designated for 2026 can be made through April 15, 2027 (2026 Form 5498 instructions, 219(f)(3)), though whether the plan or custodian will process and code it that way varies by plan.
8. Steps: confirm the son's 2026 taxable compensation and that he has been the beneficiary throughout; he opens his own Roth IRA; request a direct rollover of up to $7,500 less his other 2026 IRA contributions; repeat each year until the $35,000 lifetime cap is reached; check state tax; plan for the remaining $13,000.

## Answer A

````text
You can’t move the whole $48,000 this year. The law is real, but for 2026 a 529-to-Roth rollover counts against the annual IRA contribution limit—**$7,500** for someone under 50—and against a **$35,000 lifetime cap** per beneficiary. So the most your son can move in 2026 is **$7,500**, and only if he has at least $7,500 of earned income and hasn’t used any of his 2026 IRA room. Of the $48,000, at most $35,000 can ever go into his Roth under this rule; the other $13,000 cannot.

Your 2004 account passes the 15-year test. The 5-year lookback and the plan’s own rules still need checking.

**The 2026 number is the smallest of:**

- $7,500;
- his 2026 earned income—W-2 wages or net self-employment income;
- $7,500 minus any 2026 traditional or Roth IRA contributions he makes;
- the amount eligible under the 5-year lookback; and
- $35,000 minus any prior 529-to-Roth rollovers.

If he earned at least $7,500, made no other 2026 IRA contributions, and the plan confirms at least $7,500 is eligible, the number is $7,500. If he earned $0, it is $0. A 401(k) at work does not use this IRA room—but the 529 plan probably will not verify his income or other contributions. You have to.

**Steps to complete the 2026 rollover**

1. **Confirm his 2026 earned income and IRA contributions.** Check his pay stubs, W-2, or self-employment records, and whether he has already put money into a traditional or Roth IRA for 2026. This sets the real ceiling.

2. **Get the 529 plan’s rollover package and eligible amount.** Ask for the 529-to-Roth rollover form, whether partial rollovers are allowed, the plan’s year-end processing cutoff, and a written calculation of the amount eligible after excluding contributions made in the last five years and the earnings on them. Ask whether it needs a signature guarantee or a letter of acceptance from the Roth custodian. If the account was ever moved between 529 plans, ask whether the 15-year clock carried over. If the plan only allows a full-account rollover, don’t use it—you need a partial rollover.

3. **Open or confirm the Roth IRA in your son’s name.** The 529 beneficiary and the Roth owner must be the same person. If he already has a Roth IRA, use it; if not, he opens one at a custodian that accepts 529-to-Roth rollovers. You can help with the paperwork, but the account is his, not yours.

4. **Submit a direct trustee-to-trustee transfer request.** Put the lower of the 2026 ceiling or the plan’s eligible amount on the form—likely $7,500. The 529 plan must send the money directly to the Roth custodian. Do not request a check payable to your son, and do not take a distribution and redeposit it; the 60-day rollover rule does not apply to this move. Ask for the plan’s year-end cutoff and submit by early November, not late December.

5. **Confirm the deposit before December 31.** Check the Roth account and get written confirmation from both institutions. It should be recorded as a rollover contribution, not a regular contribution. If it hasn’t arrived by mid-December, call both institutions. If the money lands in January, it generally counts for 2027, and the 2026 room is lost.

6. **Keep the paper trail.** Save the 529 opening statement, contribution history, rollover form, confirmation letters, Form 1099-Q from the 529 plan, Form 5498 from the Roth custodian, and proof of his 2026 earned income. The 1099-Q reports the distribution; the 5498 shows the rollover. The 5498 may not arrive until May 2027, which is normal. Give your son copies and tell his tax preparer about the rollover.

**The rest of the money**

At $7,500 a year, $35,000 takes about five years: 2026–2029 at $7,500, then $5,000 in 2030 to stay under the lifetime cap. If annual limits rise, it could take fewer years. The remaining $13,000 cannot go into his Roth under this rule. It can stay in the 529 for future education, be used for another family member, or be withdrawn—but a nonqualified withdrawal taxes the earnings and adds a 10% penalty on the earnings. Taking it out and paying the tax does not create extra Roth room; the annual limit still applies. If you are considering a beneficiary change, check the 15-year clock first; it may restart.

**Three traps**

- **An indirect rollover fails.** A check to your son or a deposit into his bank account does not qualify.
- **The 5-year rule can make less than $7,500 eligible.** Don’t move first and sort it out later. If part of the balance is ineligible now because of recent contributions, waiting can cure that part—but it won’t increase the $35,000 lifetime cap.
- **State tax may not follow federal.** Some states tax the rollover or recapture prior state 529 deductions. Check your state before December.

If he has no earned income this year, the 2026 answer is $0; wait until he has wages. The $35,000 lifetime cap does not expire, but unused annual room does not carry forward.

Sources: [IRS Topic 313](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc313), [IRS Publication 590-A (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590a), [IRC §529(c)(3)(E)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/529). This is general information, not a substitute for advice from a CPA or the plan.
````

## Answer B

````text
**You can't move $48,000 this year — and you can't ever move all of it.** The 529-to-Roth rollover is real (SECURE 2.0, Section 126), but it has a hard lifetime cap and an annual cap, and your plan runs into both.

## The limits that decide this

| Rule | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| **$35,000 lifetime cap per beneficiary** | The most you can ever roll over is $35,000, across all his 529 accounts. Not indexed for inflation. |
| **Annual Roth limit** | $7,500 for 2026 (under age 50). The rollover counts against it, not on top of it. |
| **Earned income** | He must have earned income at least equal to the amount rolled over. No income, no rollover that year. |
| **15-year account age** | Opened 2004, so met — unless you changed the beneficiary at some point, which can restart the clock. |
| **5-year contribution rule** | Money contributed in the last five years, and the earnings on it, is ineligible. The eligible amount may be under $48,000. |
| **Direct transfer** | Trustee-to-trustee only. A check to him and a contribution is not a rollover. |
| **Roth ownership** | The Roth must be in his name; he must be the 529 beneficiary. |
| **Income limits** | Do not apply to this rollover — only the annual amount and his earned income. |

So the realistic path is **$7,500 this year**, then roughly $7,500 a year until the $35,000 cap is used — about five years. At least $13,000 of the $48,000 can never be rolled over.

## Steps

1. **Get the 529 facts.** Call the plan and ask for: the account opening date, whether he has been the beneficiary since 2004, the contribution dates and amounts (especially the last five years), the current balance, and whether the plan processes 529-to-Roth rollovers and what form it requires.
2. **Confirm his 2026 earned income.** W-2 wages, self-employment net earnings, etc. The rollover cannot exceed it. If he has none, this waits.
3. **Set the 2026 number.** The smallest of: $7,500; his 2026 earned income; and the portion of the 529 that has been in the account at least five years.
4. **Have him open a Roth IRA** in his own name at a custodian that accepts 529 rollovers. Get the account number and the custodian's transfer instructions.
5. **Submit the plan's direct trustee-to-trustee rollover form**, with the Roth account number and custodian. Do not have the 529 send him a check — that becomes a non-qualified withdrawal (tax plus 10% penalty on earnings) and an excess Roth contribution (6% excise tax per year).
6. **Finish by December 31, 2026** so it counts against the 2026 limit. Confirm both the 529 plan and the Roth custodian processed it.
7. **Keep the paper trail:** the 1099-Q from the 529, Form 5498 (Box 10) from the Roth custodian, and the plan statements.
8. **Repeat each year** — 2027, 2028, and so on — until the $35,000 lifetime cap is used, subject to his earned income each year. If he also contributes to a Roth from his paycheck, the $7,500 is the combined cap, not extra room.
9. **Decide the rest.** At least $13,000 stays in the 529. Options: graduate school, change the beneficiary to a future grandchild, student loan repayment up to $10,000 lifetime, K-12 tuition, or a non-qualified withdrawal (earnings taxed plus 10% penalty; contributions come out tax-free).

## Check before you start

- **State tax.** Some states don't treat the rollover as a qualified 529 distribution and may recapture prior deductions or tax the earnings. Ask the plan or a tax advisor about your state.
- **The 5-year rule.** If you contributed in 2021–2026, that money isn't eligible. The plan may not track it for you; you may need your own records.
- **Beneficiary changes.** If you ever changed the beneficiary, the 15-year clock may have restarted. Confirm.
- **Plan rules and timing.** Not every 529 plan has its rollover process running smoothly yet. Ask for the form and expected timeline, and start soon — December is a busy month for processors.

Sources: [Savingforcollege.com — 529 to Roth IRA rollover rules (updated Aug. 2026)](https://www.savingforcollege.com/article/roll-over-529-plan-funds-to-a-roth-ira), [IRAR — 2025–2026 IRA contribution limits](https://www.iraresources.com/ira-contribution-limits).
````
