# PAIR p058

## Request

````text
My son just finished college with $48,000 left in his 529 plan, which I opened when he was born in 2004. I read that the new law lets you roll leftover 529 money into a Roth IRA tax-free, so I'm planning to move the whole $48,000 into a Roth IRA in his name this year. Can you write out the steps to do the rollover before December?
````

## Answer key

# Answer key: roll the whole $48,000 529 into a Roth IRA this year? (written before the runs, verified 2026-10-22/23; sources in sources/en-529-roth.md)

The premise is wrong. The SECURE 2.0 rollover (IRC 529(c)(3)(E), Pub. L. 117-328 section 126, for distributions after December 31, 2023) does not allow $48,000 in one year, and the Roth must be the son's:
1. Annual cap: the son's 408A(c)(2) limit, which is the section 219 maximum: the lesser of $7,500 for 2026 (IR-2025-111) and his taxable compensation. His other IRA contributions for the year reduce it.
2. Lifetime cap: $35,000 per beneficiary, all years combined (Pub 590-A). At least $13,000 can never reach the son's Roth this way.
3. Only contributions made more than 5 years before the transfer, plus their earnings, qualify (Pub 970).
4. The transfer must be a direct trustee-to-trustee transfer into a Roth "maintained for the benefit of" the beneficiary. That means the son owns it, and a check paid out to the family fails.
5. "Tax-free" is federal only. States vary.

What holds: the account was opened in 2004 (about 21-22 years ago), so it passes the 15-year test if the son has been the beneficiary throughout. Roth income limits do not reduce the rollover (408A(c)(3)(E)).

Correct figures:
- Lifetime: 48,000 − 35,000 = $13,000 stays outside, at best.
- 2026 maximum = smaller of (a) min($7,500, his 2026 taxable compensation) minus his other 2026 IRA contributions, and (b) contributions older than 5 years plus their earnings.
  - Ample wages, $2,000 into his own Roth: 7,500 − 2,000 = $5,500.
  - He earns $4,000 and makes no other contribution: $4,000.
  - He earns $4,000 and contributes $2,000: min(7,500, 4,000) − 2,000 = $2,000, not $4,000.
- Full $35,000: 7,500 × 4 = 30,000 (2026-2029) + 5,000 in 2030 = at least five tax years. Later limits (indexed in $500 steps) are not yet announced. At 8,000 × 4 = 32,000 < 35,000, it still takes five. The 2025 slot closed April 15, 2026.
- All $48,000 in 2026: at least 48,000 − 7,500 = $40,500 falls outside the rule (more if his compensation, older money or room is below $7,500). The IRS has not described this exact case:
  - Roth side: 6% excise for each year the excess stays. Withdrawing it with earnings by the 2026 return due date (including extensions) avoids only this 6%.
  - 529 side: the earnings portion of the non-qualifying amount is still taxable, generally plus the 10% additional tax.

Best answer: corrects the premise first and shows the arithmetic. Its steps: confirm the son's compensation and beneficiary history; he opens his own Roth; request a direct rollover of up to $7,500, less his other IRA contributions; repeat yearly; check state tax; plan for the $13,000. Says December is not the federal deadline. Moderate length.

Side-claim traps (any added claim must be right):
- Limits: 2026 is $7,500 (2025: $7,000). The 50+ catch-up of $1,100 ($8,600) does not apply to the son. The rollover counts against his regular Roth limit (408A(c)(5)(B)(ii)).
- Income:
  - The 2026 phase-outs ($153,000-$168,000 single; $242,000-$252,000 joint) apply only to regular contributions.
  - "Earned income" for "taxable compensation" is acceptable. "No income needed" is wrong.
- Deadline:
  - The 2026 Form 5498 instructions and 219(f)(3) allow a rollover designated for 2026 through April 15, 2027.
  - Whether the plan or custodian will process and code it that way **varies by plan**.
  - "Finish by December to be safe" is fine. December as the legal deadline is a minor error.
- State tax (**varies by place**):
  - California does not conform: the rollover is includible, plus a 2.5% additional tax. AB 2444 (conformity) was "held under submission" May 14, 2026 per a secondary source; no enactment found.
  - Whether California taxes the whole rollover or only the earnings is **unresolved**. Accept either reading, but not a California dollar figure stated as certain.
  - New York treats rollovers from its own plan as qualified from September 5, 2024.
  - Other states were not checked; recapture of state deductions is possible.
- Beneficiary change:
  - A change to a family member is free of income tax, and the new beneficiary gets a separate $35,000 cap.
  - Whether the change restarts the 15-year clock is **unresolved** (likewise an incoming 529 rollover or owner change). No IRS guidance exists (industry request, September 2023). The Priority Guidance Plan's §529 item (2025-2026 item 21; 2026-2027 item 25) concerns 2025-law implementation, not this.
  - Stating either answer as settled, or claiming the IRS is drafting guidance on it, is wrong.
- Mechanics:
  - Indirect rollovers fail, and the Roth must exist first.
  - Acceptance letters, forms and processing times **vary by plan**.
  - The plan issues Form 1099-Q. The custodian reports the rollover in Form 5498 box 10.
- The $13,000:
  - Student loans: up to $10,000 lifetime for the son, plus a separate $10,000 per sibling. One shared $10,000 is a minor error.
  - Change the beneficiary.
  - Use it for future qualified education, including graduate school and, since July 4, 2025, postsecondary credentials.
  - A nonqualified withdrawal taxes only the earnings, plus 10%. "The whole withdrawal is taxed" is wrong.
- **Not verified** (do not grade unless plainly wrong): the Roth's own 5-year and ordering rules for later withdrawals; spousal compensation supporting the rollover.

Deductions:
- Major:
  - Endorsing $48,000, or more than the year's cap, in 2026.
  - A parent-owned Roth or an indirect rollover.
  - Missing the annual cap, the $35,000 cap or the compensation requirement.
  - Wrong arithmetic.
  - A settled rule on the 15-year reset.
- Minor:
  - December as the federal deadline.
  - "Tax-free" with no state caveat.
  - Excessive length.

## Key points

1. Premise correction: the whole $48,000 cannot be rolled into a Roth in one year. The SECURE 2.0 529-to-Roth rollover (IRC 529(c)(3)(E), Pub. L. 117-328 section 126, distributions after December 31, 2023) is capped each year at the son's Roth limit (408A(c)(2), the section 219 maximum): the lesser of $7,500 for 2026 (IR-2025-111) and his taxable compensation, reduced by his other IRA contributions for the year (e.g. ample wages and $2,000 into his own Roth gives $5,500; $4,000 earned and $2,000 contributed gives $2,000).
2. Lifetime cap: $35,000 per beneficiary, all years combined (Pub 590-A), so at least $13,000 (48,000 - 35,000) can never reach the son's Roth this way, and moving the full $35,000 takes at least five tax years (7,500 x 4 = 30,000 for 2026-2029, plus 5,000 in 2030).
3. The Roth must be the son's own, opened by him before the transfer, and the money must move by direct trustee-to-trustee transfer into a Roth maintained for the benefit of the beneficiary; a check paid out to the family (an indirect rollover) or a parent-owned Roth fails.
4. Only 529 contributions made more than 5 years before the transfer, plus their earnings, qualify (Pub 970). The account opened in 2004 (about 21-22 years ago) passes the 15-year test if the son has been the beneficiary throughout, and Roth income limits do not reduce the rollover (408A(c)(3)(E)).
5. Moving all $48,000 in 2026 would put at least $40,500 (48,000 - 7,500) outside the rule: on the Roth side a 6% excise tax for each year the excess stays (withdrawing it with earnings by the 2026 return due date, including extensions, avoids only the 6%), and on the 529 side the earnings portion of the non-qualifying amount is taxable, generally plus the 10% additional tax.
6. "Tax-free" is federal only; state treatment varies, so the state tax treatment must be checked.
7. December is not the federal deadline: a rollover designated for 2026 can be made through April 15, 2027 (2026 Form 5498 instructions, 219(f)(3)), though whether the plan or custodian will process and code it that way varies by plan.
8. Steps: confirm the son's 2026 taxable compensation and that he has been the beneficiary throughout; he opens his own Roth IRA; request a direct rollover of up to $7,500 less his other 2026 IRA contributions; repeat each year until the $35,000 lifetime cap is reached; check state tax; plan for the remaining $13,000.

## Answer A

````text
**Bottom line:** The rule is real, but it does not allow $48,000 in one year. The 529-to-Roth rollover is capped at **$7,500 for 2026** and **$35,000 over your son's lifetime**. Of the $48,000, at most $35,000 can ever go into his Roth this way. The other $13,000 cannot.

**The limits that apply**

- **Annual limit: $7,500 for 2026.** The rollover counts as a Roth IRA contribution. The 2026 IRA limit is $7,500 for someone under 50, so $7,500 is the most you can move this year.
- **Lifetime limit: $35,000 per beneficiary.** This is a lifetime cap across all 529 accounts for your son. It is not a per-year cap, and it is not indexed for inflation.
- **Five-year rule.** Only money that has been in the 529 for at least five years is eligible. Contributions made in the last five years, and the earnings on them, are excluded. The plan can tell you the balance from five years ago.
- **Earned income.** Your son must have 2026 earned income at least equal to the amount rolled over. Wages, salary, tips, and self-employment income count; investment income and scholarships do not. If he earned $4,000, the max is $4,000. If he earned nothing, no rollover this year.
- **15-year rule.** The account must have been maintained for him for at least 15 years. Opened in 2004, so this is satisfied — unless the account was rolled over to a different 529 plan or the beneficiary was changed. Ask the plan to confirm.

The 529-to-Roth rollover also bypasses the usual Roth IRA income limits. That is not a problem here, but it is useful to know.

**The realistic schedule**

Assuming the annual limit stays at $7,500 and the five-year rule does not reduce the eligible amount:

| Year | Rollover | Cumulative |
|------|----------|------------|
| 2026 | $7,500 | $7,500 |
| 2027 | $7,500 | $15,000 |
| 2028 | $7,500 | $22,500 |
| 2029 | $7,500 | $30,000 |
| 2030 | $5,000 | $35,000 |

The limits may rise with inflation, which would shorten the schedule. The five-year rule may reduce the eligible amount, which would lengthen it.

**Steps for the 2026 rollover**

1. **Confirm his 2026 earned income.** Use pay stubs, a W-2, or self-employment records. The rollover cannot exceed his compensation. If he has no earned income, stop and wait until he does.

2. **Call the 529 plan.** Ask:
   - "What was the account balance five years ago?"
   - "How much of the current balance comes from contributions made in the last five years?"
   - "Has the account been maintained for this beneficiary for 15 years, including any plan-to-plan rollovers or beneficiary changes?"
   - "Do you allow partial rollovers, and what is your 529-to-Roth rollover form?"
   - "What do you need from the Roth custodian, and is there a fee?"

3. **Open a Roth IRA in your son's name.** He is an adult, so he owns the account and must sign the application. Any brokerage works, but confirm it accepts 529-to-Roth rollovers. Make sure it is a Roth IRA, not a traditional IRA. Have the account number and custodian details ready.

4. **Complete the 529 plan's direct-rollover form.** Specify the amount — no more than $7,500. The form may require the Roth account number, the custodian's address, your son's SSN, and a signature. Some plans also require a signature guarantee or a signature from the Roth custodian.

5. **Request a direct trustee-to-trustee transfer.** The money must go from the 529 plan to the Roth custodian. Do not have the check made out to you or to your son. If the money comes to either of you, the earnings portion is taxable and may be subject to the 10% penalty, and you cannot fix it by writing a check to the Roth afterward.

6. **Roll over the smallest of these three numbers:** $7,500, his 2026 earned income, and the five-year-eligible amount.

7. **Keep the tax forms.** The 529 plan will send a Form 1099-Q. The Roth custodian will send a Form 5498. Keep both. A correctly done rollover is not taxable federally, but the IRS will see the 1099-Q. If you are not sure how to report it, ask a tax preparer.

8. **Do not double up.** The $7,500 counts against his 2026 Roth IRA contribution limit. If he also contributes from his paycheck, the total cannot exceed $7,500 (or his earned income, if lower).

9. **Check state tax.** Some states do not follow the federal treatment, and some may recapture a state tax deduction you claimed. Check with your state's plan or a tax advisor.

**Timing**

- Complete the transfer by **December 31, 2026** to count against the 2026 limit. "Before December" is fine and gives the plan time to process.
- The annual limit does not carry over. If you skip a year, you lose that year's capacity.
- There is no deadline for the $35,000 lifetime limit. You can spread the rollovers over several years.
- Some plans take two to four weeks. Start now.

**What about the other $13,000?**

It can stay in the 529. Options:
- Save it for graduate school, professional school, or an apprenticeship.
- Use it for qualified expenses: tuition, fees, books, supplies, equipment, student loan repayment (up to $10,000 lifetime), or K-12 tuition (up to $10,000 a year).
- Change the beneficiary to another family member, such as a future grandchild. Check the 15-year clock for the new beneficiary.
- Take a non-qualified withdrawal. You would pay tax on the earnings plus a 10% penalty; the contributions come out tax-free.

**One note on the Roth side:** The rolled-over money is treated as a Roth contribution. The earnings follow the Roth 5-year and age-59½ rules, so he should not withdraw them anytime soon. The contributions themselves can be withdrawn tax-free at any time.

**Sources:** [IRS 2026 IRA limit](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/401k-limit-increases-to-24500-for-2026-ira-limit-increases-to-7500), [Savingforcollege 2026 guide](https://www.savingforcollege.com/article/roll-over-529-plan-funds-to-a-roth-ira), [T. Rowe Price](https://www.troweprice.com/en/us/insights/five-things-to-know-about-529-to-roth-ira-rollovers).
````

## Answer B

````text
You can't move the whole $48,000. The most you can move in 2026 is **$7,500**, and **$13,000 of the $48,000 can never go into the Roth** under current law.

The 529-to-Roth rollover comes from SECURE 2.0 and has been available since 2024. It has several independent limits, and the annual one binds this year:

| Rule | 2026 |
|---|---|
| Lifetime cap per beneficiary | **$35,000** |
| Annual cap (under age 50) | **$7,500** |
| Eligible funds | 529 contributions made more than 5 years before the transfer, plus their earnings |
| Earned income | Your son's 2026 compensation must be at least the amount rolled over |
| 15-year rule | Account open 15+ years — satisfied (opened 2004) |
| Transfer method | Direct trustee-to-trustee only |

So the 2026 rollover is the smallest of: **$7,500**, the plan's Roth-eligible amount, his 2026 earned income, and **$35,000 minus any prior 529-to-Roth rollovers**. If he also contributes to an IRA from his paycheck this year, subtract that too. His income level doesn't disqualify the rollover the way it would a regular Roth contribution ([IRS 2026 limits](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/401k-limit-increases-to-24500-for-2026-ira-limit-increases-to-7500), [Savingforcollege rules](https://www.savingforcollege.com/article/roll-over-529-plan-funds-to-a-roth-ira)).

**Three checks before you file anything**

1. **The plan's Roth-eligible amount.** Only contributions made before roughly late 2021 — five years before a 2026 transfer — plus the earnings on them qualify. If you contributed in the last five years, the eligible amount is lower than $48,000. Ask the plan for its Roth-eligible calculation and contribution history.

2. **His 2026 earned income.** W-2 wages and net self-employment earnings count. Scholarships, gifts, and investment income do not. If he earned $4,000, the max is $4,000. If he earned nothing, the 2026 rollover is $0 — the 529 can wait until he has wages.

3. **Your state's tax treatment.** Most states follow the federal treatment, but not all. A late-2025 survey found 30 states follow federal; Indiana, Louisiana, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Utah, and Vermont recapture state 529 deductions; California taxes the earnings plus a 2.5% penalty; Colorado, Missouri, and New Jersey were still deciding. Check your state before transferring ([state-by-state summary](https://praetorianguard.biz/resources/slott-reports/state-tax-treatment-of-529-to-roth-ira-rollovers/)).

**The 2026 rollover, step by step**

1. **Set the amount.** Use the smallest figure from the list above. Confirm it in writing with the 529 plan.

2. **Open a Roth IRA in his name**, not yours, at a custodian that accepts 529 rollovers — or use his existing Roth. He is 22, so he can open it himself; you can still initiate the 529 side as the account owner.

3. **Get the 529 plan's rollover form.** Ask for a "direct trustee-to-trustee rollover to a Roth IRA." Put the Roth account number and custodian on the form. The plan may require a letter of acceptance from the custodian, a signature guarantee, or your son's signature. Do not request a check payable to him or to you — that would be a taxable nonqualified withdrawal, and the 60-day rollover rule does not apply to 529-to-Roth transfers.

4. **Submit by early November.** Processing can take several weeks. The transfer must be completed by December 31, 2026 to count against the 2026 annual limit. There is no separate December 31 deadline for the rollover itself; if you miss it, it becomes a 2027 rollover.

5. **Confirm the deposit and the reporting.** The Roth custodian should report the amount on Form 5498, box 10 (Roth IRA contributions). The 529 plan will issue Form 1099-Q to you as account owner. Keep the rollover form, the confirmation, the 1099-Q, the 5498, and the plan's contribution records.

6. **File the returns.** If the requirements are met, the rollover is not taxable federally. Don't report the 1099-Q as income; be ready to document that it was a direct rollover. Your state return may differ.

7. **Plan the rest.** Repeat up to $7,500 (or the then-current limit) each year until the $35,000 lifetime cap. The remaining $13,000 can stay for graduate school or other qualified expenses, change beneficiary (though that may restart the 15-year clock for a future Roth rollover), or be withdrawn nonqualified — tax plus a 10% penalty on the earnings.

**Traps**

- The annual limit is use-it-or-lose-it; it does not carry over.
- The lifetime cap is per beneficiary across all 529 accounts, not per plan.
- If you transfer more than the eligible amount, the excess is a nonqualified withdrawal — tax and 10% penalty on the earnings.
- A bill introduced in the Senate on September 24, 2026 ([S. 5550](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119s5550is/html/BILLS-119s5550is.htm)) would eliminate the $35,000 lifetime cap, but it has only been referred to the Finance Committee. It is not law; don't plan around it.
- If the 529 was moved between plans or the beneficiary was changed, confirm the 15-year clock carried over.
````
