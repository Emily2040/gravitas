Don’t leave it alone. That premise is the expensive part: a health FSA is not an HSA. It is generally **use-it-or-lose-it**. Your plan may offer a limited carryover—**$660 for a 2025 plan year, $680 for 2026**—or a grace period of up to 2½ months, but many plans offer neither, and a plan generally cannot offer both ([IRS Pub. 969](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p969); [2026 limits](https://blogs.payroll.org/pay-news-now/irs-announces-2026-colas-for-transportation-fringes-fsa-deferrals)). So do not assume the full $1,900 follows you to March.

**Call HR/benefits today and ask four things:**
1. Does our plan have a carryover, a grace period, or neither?
2. What is the exact carryover cap or grace-period end date?
3. What is the deadline to submit claims for expenses incurred this year?
4. Is the full $1,900 available now, or only what I’ve contributed so far? Health FSAs usually make the full annual election available from day one.

**Then follow your plan’s situation:**

- **No carryover and no grace period:** Spend the full $1,900 by December 31 on eligible expenses. Book dental now and ask for a treatment plan and pre-treatment estimate. Schedule as much covered work as possible in December—cleanings, exams, fillings, crowns, root canals, extractions, dentures, orthodontia. Also use eligible vision, prescription, copay, and deductible expenses if dental can’t absorb it all.

- **Carryover only:** Only the cap survives. Spend at least **$1,900 minus the cap** before December 31. If your March dental work will cost at least the cap, leaving the cap is reasonable; otherwise leave only what you will definitely use. Unused carryover still expires later.

- **Grace period:** A March appointment may be covered if the service date falls within the grace period—typically through March 15. Confirm the exact date. If your appointment is after it, treat the money as use-it-or-lose-it by December 31.

**Do not prepay for March services just to claim the expense this year.** FSA reimbursement generally follows the date the service is provided, not the date you pay a deposit. Get the dentist’s date and estimate in writing, and keep receipts and explanation-of-benefits statements for claims.

The practical move: **schedule dental treatment in December if you can, spend down to the carryover cap if you have one, and submit claims before the run-out deadline.** Leaving the full $1,900 untouched risks forfeiting roughly $1,220–$1,240.
