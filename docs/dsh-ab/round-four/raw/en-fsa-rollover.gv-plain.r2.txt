## First, a correction that changes the plan

**An FSA is not an HSA.** HSA money is yours forever, rolls over without limit, and can be invested. Health FSA money is use-it-or-lose-it, with only a *capped* carryover — at most **$680** for 2026 plan years ($660 for 2025), and only if your employer's plan adopts carryover at all ([2026 limits](https://lhdbenefits.com/resources/2026-fsa-limits/), IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32).

So the realistic math on leaving $1,900 alone:

| Outcome | Amount |
|---|---|
| Carried into next year (best case) | ~$680 |
| **Forfeited** | **~$1,220** |

And the carryover only happens if you're still employed and still enrolled in the FSA in the new plan year. If you change jobs, the whole balance — carryover included — is gone.

One nuance worth checking: some plans use a **2.5-month grace period** (through ~March 15) instead of carryover. Plans can offer carryover *or* grace period, not both. If yours is a grace-period plan, your March dental work might be reimbursable from *this* year's money — which would solve most of your problem. That's the single most important thing to find out.

## This week: three questions for HR or the plan administrator

1. **Carryover or grace period — and how much?** Get the exact dollar cap and whether it's automatic.
2. **What's the claims run-out deadline?** The service must happen by Dec 31 (or grace-period end), but you usually have until March–April to submit receipts.
3. **Is my full $1,900 available now?** Under the uniform-coverage rule it should be, even though it's deducted from your paychecks all year — meaning you can spend it before it's fully funded.

## Then: spend it before Dec 31, not in March

Move the dental work **into December** if the dentist has an opening — cleanings, fillings, crowns, and any planned work. Also book now:

- **Vision** — exam, glasses, extra pairs, contacts, LASIK (eligible)
- **Dental** — the March work, plus a cleaning for you and any dependents
- **Medical** — meet your deductible, prescriptions in bulk, physical therapy, chiropractic, acupuncture, mental health visits
- **OTC and supplies** — first aid, sunscreen, menstrual products, contact solution, bandages, thermometers, blood-pressure cuffs, breast pumps, hearing aids
- **Dependents' expenses** — their copays, braces, and glasses all count
- **Known upcoming costs** — refill 90-day prescriptions before Dec 31

## Two traps to avoid

- **Don't buy junk to burn the balance.** Vitamins and supplements generally aren't eligible unless prescribed for a diagnosed condition, and a denial leaves you back where you started.
- **Don't assume the carryover is safe.** Confirm it in writing. A verbal "yeah, we roll over" from a coworker is how people lose $1,200.

If it turns out you have neither carryover nor grace period, your only move is to spend aggressively in December — schedule the dental work now and use the balance on vision, prescriptions, and dependents' care.

This is general information, not tax or benefits advice — your plan document is the authority, so get the carryover and run-out rules confirmed by your administrator in writing.
