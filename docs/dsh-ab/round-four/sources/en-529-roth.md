# 529 to Roth IRA rollover: source check

Checked 2026-10-22. Every URL below was opened on 2026-10-22. Quotes are verbatim, in the original English.
Primary sources: the Internal Revenue Code (LII/Cornell U.S. Code text and its statutory notes), IRS Pub 590-A (2025), IRS Pub 970 (2025), IRS news release IR-2025-111, Instructions for Forms 1099-R and 5498 (2026), the Treasury/IRS 2025-2026 Priority Guidance Plan, California FTB 3805P instructions (2025), and New York IT-201-I (2025). One state-plan document (the NC 529 Plan FAQ) is used only for the open questions, because the IRS has published nothing on them.

The request: a parent opened a 529 in 2004 when the son was born. The son has just graduated, and $48,000 is left. The parent wants to move all $48,000 into a Roth IRA in the son's name "this year", before December, tax-free.

---

## Items checked

### 1. SECURE 2.0 Act section 126 created IRC 529(c)(3)(E): **confirmed**
- URL: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/529 (statute text) and https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/408A (statutory notes)
- Quote (26 U.S.C. 529(c)(3)(E) heading): "(E)Special rollover to roth iras from long-term qualified tuition programs"
- Quote (408A notes): "Pub. L. 117–328, div. T, title I, § 126(d), Dec. 29, 2022, 136 Stat. 5317, provided that:"
- Note: section 126 also amended 408A: (c)(3)(E), (c)(5)(B)(ii) and (e)(1)(C).

### 2. The 529 account must have been open at least 15 years: **confirmed**
- URL: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/529 ; https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590a
- Quote (statute): "which has been maintained for the 15-year period ending on the date of such distribution"
- Quote (Pub 590-A, 2025): "The rollover must be from a section 529 account that has been open for more than 15 years."
- In this case: an account opened in 2004 has been open about 22 years in 2026, so the test is met as long as it is the same account and the same beneficiary (see item 12).

### 3. The money must go to a Roth IRA of the designated beneficiary: **confirmed**
- URL: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/529 ; https://cfnc.org/media/jtoc3gqz/rollover-to-roth-ira-faq.pdf (NC 529 Plan FAQ, undated)
- Quote (statute): "(II)is paid in a direct trustee-to-trustee transfer to a Roth IRA maintained for the benefit of such designated beneficiary."
- Quote (NC 529 FAQ 12): "it is required that the beneficiary of the 529 Account be the same as the owner of the Roth IRA account."
- In this case: the son (22) must open and own the Roth IRA. The parent cannot hold it for him.

### 4. Lifetime limit $35,000: **confirmed** (per beneficiary, all years combined)
- URL: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/529 ; https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590a
- Quote (statute): "the aggregate amount of such distributions with respect to the designated beneficiary for such taxable year and all prior taxable years exceeds $35,000."
- Quote (Pub 590-A): "all such rollovers for the individual’s lifetime cannot exceed $35,000."

### 5. Yearly cap equals the annual IRA contribution limit, reduced by the beneficiary's other IRA contributions; 2026 figure: **confirmed, with the 2026 figure supplied ($7,500)**
- URL (rule): https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/529
- Quote: "does not exceed the amount applicable to the designated beneficiary under section 408A(c)(2) for the taxable year (reduced by the amount of aggregate contributions made during the taxable year to all individual retirement plans maintained for the benefit of the designated beneficiary)."
- URL (2026 figure): https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/401k-limit-increases-to-24500-for-2026-ira-limit-increases-to-7500 (IR-2025-111, Nov. 13, 2025)
- Quote: "The limit on annual contributions to an IRA is increased to $7,500 from $7,000."
- The 50-and-over catch-up rises to $1,100 for 2026, making $8,600. It does not apply to a 22-year-old.
- 2025 was $7,000. Confirmed in Pub 590-A (2025): "$7,000 ($8,000 if you are age 50 or older)".
- Related: 408A(c)(5)(B)(ii) makes the 529 rollover count against the regular Roth limit. Quote: "Clause (i) shall not apply to any qualified rollover contribution described in subsection (e)(1)(C)." (https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/408A)

### 6. The beneficiary needs earned income at least equal to the rollover: **confirmed by the statute; the wording is corrected to "taxable compensation"**
- The rule chain: 529(c)(3)(E)(ii)(I) caps the rollover at the 408A(c)(2) amount. 408A(c)(2) is the maximum deduction under section 219. Section 219(b)(1) caps that deduction at the lesser of the dollar limit or the person's compensation.
- URL: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/219
- Quote (219(b)(1)(B)): "an amount equal to the compensation includible in the individual's gross income for such taxable year."
- URL: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590a
- Quote (Pub 590-A, Roth limit): "your contribution limit is generally the lesser of: $7,000 ($8,000 if you are age 50 or older), or Your taxable compensation."
- Note: the 529-rollover paragraphs in IRS Pubs 590-A and 970 do not mention compensation. The requirement comes from the statutory cross-reference. The correct term is "taxable compensation" (wages, self-employment income and similar), not "earned income" in the EITC sense.

### 7. Contributions made in the last 5 years, and their earnings, are not eligible: **confirmed**
- URL: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/529 ; https://www.irs.gov/publications/p970
- Quote (statute): "(I)does not exceed the aggregate amount contributed to the program (and earnings attributable thereto) before the 5-year period ending on the date of the distribution"
- Quote (Pub 970, 2025): "does not exceed total contributions (and attributable earnings) made to the QTP more than 5 years before the distribution date."
- The IRS has not said how plans should track which earnings belong to recent contributions. NC 529 FAQ 6: "The NC 529 Plan does not have any guidance from the IRS on this five-year requirement."

### 8. It must be a direct trustee-to-trustee transfer: **confirmed**
- URL: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590a ; https://cfnc.org/media/jtoc3gqz/rollover-to-roth-ira-faq.pdf
- Quote (Pub 590-A): "The rollover must be paid through a direct trustee-to-trustee transfer to a Roth IRA maintained for the benefit of the beneficiary."
- Quote (NC 529 FAQ 2): "A Roth rollover from the NC 529 Plan must go directly from the NC 529 Account to the Roth IRA trustee."
- An indirect rollover (a check to the family, deposited later) does not qualify. The Roth IRA has to exist before the transfer is requested.

### 9. Roth income limits do not apply: **confirmed** (408A(c)(3)(E) adds the income-based reduction back, up to the 529 rollover amount)
- URL: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/408A
- Quote: "The amount determined under subparagraph (A) shall be increased by the lesser of— (i)the amount of contributions described in section 529(c)(3)(E) for the taxable year, or"
- Effect: the beneficiary's MAGI phase-out does not shrink the 529 rollover. His own regular Roth contributions are still subject to it. The IRS publications do not discuss this point.
- 2026 phase-out ranges, for reference only (IR-2025-111): "between $153,000 and $168,000 for singles and heads of household"; married filing jointly "between $242,000 and $252,000".

### 10. Applies to distributions after Dec 31, 2023: **confirmed**
- URL: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/408A (notes) ; https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590a
- Quote (section 126(d)): "shall apply with respect to distributions after December 31, 2023."
- Quote (Pub 590-A): "Beginning with distributions made after December 31, 2023, a beneficiary of a section 529 qualified tuition program is permitted to roll over"

### 11. State tax treatment varies: **confirmed** (two examples with opposite treatment; other states not checked)
- California does not conform and taxes the rollover.
  - URL: https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2025/2025-3805p-instructions.html (WebFetch got 403; read in the built-in browser)
  - Quote: "California law does not conform to this federal provision. Rollover distributions from an IRC Section 529 plan to a Roth IRA is includible in California taxable income and subject to an additional tax of 2½%."
  - Caution: the FTB text says the "rollover distributions" are includible. Practitioner forums disagree on whether only the earnings portion is taxed. Not resolved here.
- New York has treated the rollover as qualified since Sept 5, 2024. Earlier rollovers were nonqualified, and prior deductions were recaptured.
  - URL: https://www.tax.ny.gov/forms/current-forms/it/it201i.htm (2025 IT-201-I, line 22)
  - Quote: the excluded uses include a withdrawal "to make a rollover to a Roth IRA as provided for in IRC § 529(c)(3)(E) on or after September 5, 2024,"
- Other states: could not verify (not checked). Each state's treatment, including recapture of state deductions, has to be checked for the state of residence and the state whose plan it is.

### 12. Does a change of beneficiary restart the 15-year clock? **could not verify: no IRS guidance found as of 2026-10-22**
- URL: https://cfnc.org/media/jtoc3gqz/rollover-to-roth-ira-faq.pdf (NC 529 Plan FAQ 4; undated, written for the January 2024 launch)
- Quote: "The NC 529 Plan does not have any guidance from the IRS on whether a change of beneficiary resets the 15-year requirement. The 529 industry submitted a letter to the IRS in September 2023 seeking guidance"
- URL: https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-counsel/2025-2026-initial-pgp.pdf (Treasury/IRS Priority Guidance Plan, Sept 30, 2025)
- Quote (item 21): "Guidance under §529 regarding enhancements to qualified tuition programs."
- The 2025 IRS Pubs 590-A and 970 are silent on the question, and searches found no notice or regulation. The statute says only that the program "of a designated beneficiary" must have been "maintained for the 15-year period". The same open question applies to a rollover from another 529 plan and to a change of account owner (NC FAQ 3 and 5).
- In this case: if the son has been the only beneficiary since 2004, the question does not arise. If the beneficiary was ever changed, an answer must flag the uncertainty and must not state a rule.

## Facts a correct answer needs that were missing from the list

### 13. December is not the federal cutoff for the 2026 slot: **confirmed for 2025 in IRS text; 2026 follows from the 2026 Form 5498 instructions**
- URL: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590a
- Quote: "A distribution made after December 31, 2025, and before April 15, 2026, that is rolled over to a Roth IRA by April 15, 2026, and designated for 2025 would be reported as a Roth IRA contribution for 2025."
- URL: https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099r (2026, Form 5498 box 10)
- Quote: "in 2026 and through April 15, 2027, designated for 2026. Also enter qualified rollover contributions made from a section 529 qualified tuition program (QTP) to a Roth IRA that are designated for 2026."
- Caveat (varies by plan): plan and custodian processing times, and whether they will accept a prior-year designation, are their own practice. The 15-year and 5-year tests are measured on the actual distribution date.

### 14. What happens if the whole $48,000 goes anyway: **confirmed from the statute and Pubs; the IRS has not described this exact over-limit case**
- Any amount above the year's cap, or above the $35,000 lifetime cap, is outside 529(c)(3)(E). That portion counts as an ordinary 529 distribution that was not used for education, and inside the Roth it is an excess contribution.
- URL: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590a
- Quote: "A 6% excise tax applies to any excess contribution to a Roth IRA."
- URL: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p970
- Quote: "Generally, if you receive a taxable distribution, you must also pay a 10% additional tax on the amount included in income."

### 15. Options for the $13,000 that can never move to a Roth: **confirmed**
- Student loans: up to $10,000 lifetime, for the beneficiary or a sibling. URL: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/529. Quote: "shall not exceed $10,000 (reduced by the amount of distributions so treated for all prior taxable years)."
- Change the beneficiary to a family member, with no income tax. URL: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p970. Quote: "There are no income tax consequences if the designated beneficiary of an account is changed to a member of the beneficiary’s family."
- Keep it for later qualified education costs (for example graduate school), or withdraw it. Only the earnings portion of a withdrawal is taxed, plus the 10% additional tax (item 14).

### 16. Tax forms: **confirmed**
- The 529 plan issues Form 1099-Q. Pub 970: "You will receive a Form 1099-Q from each of the programs from which you received a QTP distribution"
- The Roth custodian reports the rollover in Form 5498 box 10 (item 13 quote).

### 17. No minimum age for the beneficiary; plan procedures differ: **confirmed (plan-level source)**
- NC 529 FAQ 12: "There is no age requirement of the beneficiary". NC FAQ 11 says requests are paper-only, on a Request for Withdrawal Form. Other plans differ (varies by plan).

### Not checked
- The Roth IRA's own 5-year rule and ordering rules for later withdrawals of rolled-over money. Nothing was verified, so an answer should not state a rule.
- Whether a spouse's compensation (spousal IRA, section 219(c)) can support the rollover.

## Facts for the key

**The premise and why it is wrong.** The parent plans to move "the whole $48,000" into the son's Roth IRA "this year", tax-free, before December. The law does not allow that.
1. Each year's rollover is capped at the IRA contribution limit: $7,500 for 2026 for someone under 50. The cap is reduced by any other IRA contributions the son makes for 2026, and the rollover cannot exceed his 2026 taxable compensation.
2. Rollovers for one beneficiary are capped at $35,000 over his lifetime, so $13,000 of the $48,000 can never take this route.
3. Only money contributed more than 5 years before the transfer, plus its earnings, is eligible.
4. The transfer must go directly from the 529 plan to a Roth IRA that the son owns. The parent cannot own it.
5. "Tax-free" is true federally. Some states tax the rollover (California does), and others have changed their rules (New York did on Sept 5, 2024).

**Correct figures and arithmetic**
- Lifetime: $48,000 − $35,000 = **$13,000** stays outside the Roth route. That is the best case, before the 5-year exclusion.
- 2026 maximum = the smallest of: $7,500 minus the son's other 2026 IRA contributions; his 2026 taxable compensation; and the eligible older contributions plus their earnings.
  - Example: he contributes $2,000 to his own Roth for 2026, so the rollover is at most $7,500 − $2,000 = $5,500.
  - Example: he earns only $4,000 in 2026, so the rollover is at most $4,000.
- Full $35,000 at the 2026 limit: $7,500 × 4 = $30,000 (2026–2029), plus $5,000 in 2030, = $35,000, over **five tax years**. Later limits are inflation-indexed in $500 steps and not yet known. Even at $8,000 a year it still takes five years ($8,000 × 4 = $32,000 < $35,000).
- If all $48,000 were pushed in 2026: $48,000 − $7,500 = **$40,500** falls outside the exemption. In the Roth it is an excess contribution, taxed at 6% for each year it stays. As a 529 distribution, its earnings portion is taxable and generally owes the 10% additional tax. This holds unless the excess is withdrawn by the return due date (Pub 590-A withdrawal rules).
- Account age: opened 2004, so about 22 years in 2026. The 15-year test is met if the son has been the beneficiary throughout.

**Side claims an answer is likely to add, with correct values**
- 2026 IRA limit $7,500. The 50+ catch-up is $1,100 ($8,600 total) and does not apply to the son. The 2025 limit was $7,000.
- Roth income limits do not reduce the 529 rollover. The 2026 phase-outs are $153,000–$168,000 single and $242,000–$252,000 married filing jointly. They apply only to his regular Roth contributions.
- Deadline: the federal rules allow the 2026 slot to be used through April 15, 2027 if the rollover is designated for 2026. Whether the plan and custodian will process and code it that way **varies by plan**. "Before December" is not a federal deadline.
- State tax **varies by place**. California: not conformed; the rollover is includible in income plus a 2.5% additional tax. New York: qualified from Sept 5, 2024; before that it was nonqualified, with prior deductions recaptured. Other states were not checked, and recapture of earlier state deductions is possible.
- Change of beneficiary: no income tax if the new beneficiary is a family member. Whether it restarts the 15-year clock is **unresolved**: the IRS has issued no guidance (industry request September 2023; a §529 guidance project is on the 2025-2026 Priority Guidance Plan). The same is true for a rollover in from another 529 plan and for a change of owner. An answer must not state a rule.
- Indirect rollovers (taking the money out and depositing it later) do not qualify. The son's Roth IRA must already be open, and some custodians require a letter of acceptance **(varies by plan)**.
- Forms: Form 1099-Q from the 529 plan; Form 5498 box 10 from the Roth custodian.
- Leftover $13,000: student loans up to $10,000 lifetime (son or a sibling); change of beneficiary to a family member; keep it for future qualified education; or a nonqualified withdrawal, with the earnings taxed plus the 10% additional tax.
- Paperwork, online versus paper forms, and processing times **vary by plan**.
