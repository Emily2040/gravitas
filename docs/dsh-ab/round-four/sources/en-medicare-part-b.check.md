# Adversarial check: en-medicare-part-b, "Facts for the key"

Checked: 2026-10-22. Every URL below was opened on 2026-10-22, by WebFetch or in the built-in browser (ssa.gov returns 403 to WebFetch, so its pages were read in the browser). Quotes are in English, the original language, and copied from the page.

Scope: only the "Facts for the key" section of en-medicare-part-b.md was tested. Each fact was checked against the cited source or a stronger one. Every figure was recomputed.

Limits: the WebSearch budget for this turn ran out partway through. An open-web search for 2026 amendments (an HSA rule change for Medicare enrollees, new Part B SEP rules) could not be completed. To make up for it, the current live official pages were read directly: medicare.gov, ssa.gov, cms.gov, and IRS Pub. 969 (2025 edition, the latest; it cites P.L. 119-21 of 2025-07-04 only for telehealth). None of them shows a change to any rule below. The 2027 Part B premium search did run, and a CMS 2027 fact sheet does not exist yet (404).

## The premise

**K1. The coworker's rule is the general rule, but it does not apply to her, because a GHP through her own current job gives her a Part B SEP.** STANDS
- https://www.ssa.gov/medicare/sign-up
  > "If you're 65 or older, you can enroll online for Parts A and B, or Part A only. You can delay Part B if you're already covered through an employer group health plan."

**K2. She can sign up in any month while she is still working and covered, or within 8 months after the job or the coverage ends (whichever comes first, counting from the month after), with no penalty.** STANDS
- https://www.ssa.gov/help/iClaim_medSEP.html
  > "During any month you remain covered under the group health plan and your, or your spouse's, current employment continues; or
  > In the eight-month period that begins with the month after your group health plan coverage or the current employment it is based on ends, whichever comes first."
- https://www.ssa.gov/medicare/plan/when-to-sign-up
  > "This is a "Special Enrollment Period," and there are no penalties if you sign up or add Part B during this time."
- Precision, optional: medicare.gov dates the SEP from the month after the IEP ends ("Starts: The first month after your Initial Enrollment Period ends.", https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/sign-up/when-does-medicare-coverage-start). A sign-up during the IEP is simply an IEP enrollment. There is no penalty either way, so the key's wording is still right.

**K3. She does not need to enroll in Part B or start paying the premium this month.** STANDS (K13 is the exception)
- https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/medicare-basics/working-past-65 (panel "available to everyone at the company")
  > "You can wait until you (or your spouse) stop working (or lose your health insurance, if that happens first) to sign up for Part B (Medical Insurance), and you won't pay a late enrollment penalty."

**K4. Employer size is not a condition of the SEP.** STANDS
- https://www.ssa.gov/help/iClaim_grpHealth.html
  > GHPs based on current employment "Include: Small or large employer-sponsored plans for its current employees,"
- https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/1395y. The SEP statute (42 U.S.C. 1395p(i)(1)) refers to the GHP in 1395y(b)(1)(A)(v), which is defined
  > "in section 5000(b)(1) of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, without regard to section 5000(d) of such Code."
- https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/1395p. The "large group health plan" condition appears only in the second sentence of (i)(1), which covers people under 65 (disability).

**K5. With about 400 employees (20 or more), her group plan pays first and Medicare pays second.** STANDS
- https://www.medicare.gov/health-drug-plans/coordination/who-pays-first (answer shown after "current employer" and "20 or more employees: Yes")
  > "The group health plan pays first, and Medicare pays second."
- https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/1395y ((b)(1)(A)(ii))
  > "20 or more employees for each working day in each of 20 or more calendar weeks" "in the current calendar year or the preceding calendar year."

**K6. Paying for Part B now would buy secondary coverage, and it would start her one-time Medigap open enrollment clock.** STANDS
- https://www.medicare.gov/health-drug-plans/medigap/ready-to-buy/when (answer for turning 65 and still working)
  > "Your one-time Medigap Open Enrollment Period starts once you sign up for Part B and lasts for 6 months, even if you sign up for Part B while you still have employer coverage."

## What to do this month

**K7. (1) Do not enroll in Part B yet. Confirm with the benefits administrator that the coverage is an active-employee GHP and stays primary. A 20+ employer must offer her the same benefits as younger workers.** STANDS
- https://www.ssa.gov/help/iClaim_grpHealth.html
  > "Employers with 20 or more employees are required by law to offer current workers and their spouses who are age 65 (or older) the same GHP health benefits that are provided to younger employees."
- https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/1395y ((b)(1)(A)(i)(II))
  > "shall be entitled to the same benefits under the plan under the same conditions as any such individual" "(or spouse) under age 65."
- Suggested addition (not a refutation). Medicare.gov tells people to ask the plan this directly, so step 1 should also say "ask whether the plan requires you to sign up for Part A or Part B at 65":
  > "Ask the employer that provides your health insurance if you need to sign up for Part A (Hospital Insurance) and Part B (Medical Insurance) when you turn 65."
  (same working-past-65 panel)

**K8. (2) Premium-free Part A costs nothing with about 10 years of Medicare-taxed work (hers or a spouse's). If she signs up before her birthday month, it starts the month she turns 65.** STANDS
- https://www.medicare.gov/basics/costs/medicare-costs: premium-free when you or a spouse paid Medicare taxes long enough, "generally at least 10 years."
- https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/sign-up/when-does-medicare-coverage-start
  > "If you qualify for premium-free Part A: Your Part A coverage starts the month you turn 65. (If your birthday is on the first of the month, coverage starts the month before you turn 65.)"

**K9. (2) Once she has any Medicare, her HSA contribution limit is zero.** STANDS
- https://www.irs.gov/publications/p969 (Publication 969 (2025))
  > "Beginning with the first month you are enrolled in Medicare, your contribution limit is zero."
- https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/medicare-basics/working-past-65 (panel "Why should I stop contributions before I retire?")
  > "You're not eligible to make contributions to your HSA after you have Medicare."

**K10. (2) If she defers Part A, it later reaches back up to 6 months (never before her 65th birthday month), so contributions must stop 6 months before she applies.** STANDS
- https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/sign-up/when-does-medicare-coverage-start
  > "Your Part A coverage starts 6 months back from when you sign up or when you apply for benefits from Social Security (or the Railroad Retirement Board). Coverage can't start earlier than the month you turned 65."
- https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/medicare-basics/working-past-65
  > "you and your employer should stop contributing to your HSA 6 months before you retire or apply for benefits from Social Security (or the Railroad Retirement Board)."

**K11. (3) If she already gets Social Security, she is enrolled in A and B automatically and can decline Part B. Starting Social Security at 65 or later brings Part A automatically, which ends HSA contributions.** STANDS on substance; one detail undecided
- https://www.ssa.gov/faqs/en/questions/KA-02125.html (page dated April 17, 2023)
  > "We will automatically enroll you in Original Medicare (Part A and Part B) when you become eligible."
  > "Because you must pay a premium for Part B coverage, you can turn it down."
- https://www.ssa.gov/medicare/plan/when-to-sign-up
  > "If you are age 65 or older and receive Social Security benefits, you will be automatically enrolled in Part A."
- Could not decide: whether she declines "using the card instructions." No official page opened today states that mechanism. The SSA booklet EN-05-10043 returned 403, and the browser pane was being used by another agent. Safe wording: "decline Part B by following the instructions Social Security mails with the Medicare card, or by contacting Social Security."

**K12. (4) Get the plan's creditable drug coverage notice and keep it, so she can delay Part D without penalty.** STANDS
- https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/medicare-basics/working-past-65
  > "If you aren't sure your current drug plan is considered creditable, ask your plan – they have to tell you. Your plan will also send you this information every year."
  > "You won't pay the Part D late enrollment penalty as long as you don't go more than 63 days without creditable drug coverage."

**K13. (5) If the job or the coverage ends inside her IEP (which closes 3 months after her birthday month), there is no SEP.** STANDS
- https://www.ssa.gov/help/iClaim_medSEP.html
  > "Exception: If your group health plan coverage or the employment it is based on ends during your initial enrollment period for Medicare Part B, you do not qualify for a SEP."

**K14. (6) Later, at retirement: apply about a month before coverage ends, using CMS-40B (or the online SSA Part B application) plus CMS-L564.** NEEDS CHANGE
- The "about a month earlier" timing and CMS-L564 stand:
  - https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/medicare-basics/working-past-65
    > "If your employer coverage is ending, check when your current coverage ends and sign up for Medicare about a month earlier."
  - https://www.cms.gov/medicare/cms-forms/cms-forms/cms-forms-items/cms009718 (CMS-L564, OMB 0938-0787, expires 2028-03-31)
    > "Use this form to show proof of group health plan coverage based on current employment for Medicare enrollment"
- What is wrong: CMS-40B and SSA's "Part B only" application are only for someone who already has Part A. Step 2 of the key itself tells an HSA saver to defer Part A, and that person must apply for Parts A and B together.
  - https://www.medicare.gov/basics/forms-publications-mailings/forms/enrollment
    > Request for Enrollment in Part B (CMS-40B): "Signing up for Part B when you already have Part A."
  - https://www.ssa.gov/medicare/sign-up/part-b-only
    > "If you already have Part A, you can add Part B during specific enrollment periods."
  - https://www.ssa.gov/medicare/sign-up
    > "If you're 65 or older, you can enroll online for Parts A and B, or Part A only."
- Corrected wording: "When you retire, sign up about a month before the employer coverage ends. If you already have Part A, use the online Part B application on ssa.gov or form CMS-40B. If you put off Part A as well (for example because of an HSA), apply for Parts A and B together through Social Security, online or by appointment. Either way, include CMS-L564 (you fill in Section A, your employer fills in Section B). Remember that Part A will reach back up to 6 months, so stop HSA contributions 6 months before you apply."

## Figures (recomputed)

**K18. Standard Part B premium $202.90 a month (2025: $185.00; +$17.90). Per year $202.90 x 12 = $2,434.80.** STANDS
- https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/2026-medicare-parts-b-premiums-deductibles (released 2025-11-14)
  > "The standard monthly premium for Medicare Part B enrollees will be $202.90 for 2026, an increase of $17.90 from $185.00 in 2025."
- Arithmetic: 185.00 + 17.90 = 202.90. 202.90 x 12 = 2,434.80. Correct.

**K19. If she enrolled this month (October 2026, before her November birthday month), Part B would start in November: 2 x $202.90 = $405.80 for 2026. The 2027 premium has not been announced.** STANDS
- https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/sign-up/when-does-medicare-coverage-start
  > "Before the month you turn 65 | The month you turn 65"
- Arithmetic: 2 x 202.90 = 405.80. Correct. If her birthday is November 1, Medicare treats October as her 65th month. An October sign-up then falls in "the month you turn 65" and coverage still starts the next month, November, so the total is the same.
- 2027: https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/2027-medicare-parts-b-premiums-deductibles returned HTTP 404 on 2026-10-22. A web search found only projections (the Trustees' $209.50), no CMS announcement. Do not state a 2027 figure.

**K20. Part B deductible $283. Part A inpatient deductible $1,736 per benefit period. Premium-Part A $311 or $565 a month.** STANDS
- https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/2026-medicare-parts-b-premiums-deductibles
  > "The annual deductible for all Medicare Part B beneficiaries will be $283 in 2026"
- https://www.medicare.gov/basics/costs/medicare-costs: "$1,736 for each inpatient hospital benefit period"; Part A premium "$311 or $565 each month".

**K21. Penalty example: 20% x $202.90 = $40.58; $202.90 + $40.58 = $243.48, rounded to $243.50. Each full 12-month period adds 10%. With a valid SEP the penalty is $0.** STANDS
- https://www.medicare.gov/basics/costs/medicare-costs/avoid-penalties
  > "$202.90 (2026 Part B standard premium) + $40.58 (20% [of $202.90] late enrollment penalty)" ... "$243.50 will be your Part B monthly premium for 2026."
  > "Generally, you won't have to pay a Part B penalty if you qualify for a Special Enrollment Period"
- Arithmetic: 0.20 x 202.90 = 40.58. 202.90 + 40.58 = 243.48, which rounds to 243.50 at the nearest $0.10. Correct.

**K22. Part D penalty: 1% x $38.99 = about $0.39 per uncovered month. For 14 months, 14% x $38.99 = $5.4586, which rounds to $5.50.** STANDS
- https://www.medicare.gov/health-drug-plans/part-d/basics/costs
  > "The Part D late enrollment penalty is calculated by multiplying 1% times the "national base beneficiary premium" ($38.99 in 2026)"
- Arithmetic: 0.01 x 38.99 = 0.3899. 0.14 x 38.99 = 5.4586, which rounds to 5.50 at the nearest $0.10. Correct. The $5.50 is computed, not printed on the page; the key already says so. The base premium is reset every year, so a future penalty will use that year's figure.

## Side claims

**K23. "20+ employees is required to delay Part B" is imprecise: small and large plans both qualify, and 20+ decides who pays first. Under 20 employees, Medicare pays first and the employer plan may not pay without Part B.** STANDS
- Sources as in K4 and K5. The small-employer risk is on https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/medicare-basics/working-past-65:
  > "If you don't sign up for Part A and Part B, your job-based insurance might not cover the costs for services you get."

**K24. Part A reaches back 6 months but never before her 65th month. In the IEP, Part A starts in her birthday month (the month before if her birthday is on the 1st).** STANDS. Same quotes as K8 and K10.

**K25. HSA: 2026 limits $4,400 self-only / $8,750 family, plus $1,000 at 55+, prorated. Example with Part A from 2026-11-01 (10 eligible months): $4,500 self-only, $8,125 family.** STANDS
- https://www.irs.gov/publications/p969
  > "if you have self-only HDHP coverage, you can contribute up to $4,400." / "If you have family HDHP coverage, you can contribute up to $8,750."
  > "Your contribution limit is $2,650 ($5,300 × 6 ÷ 12)." (shows that the $1,000 catch-up is prorated together with the base limit)
- Arithmetic: (4,400 + 1,000) x 10/12 = 4,500. (8,750 + 1,000) x 10/12 = 8,125. January to October is 10 months, because the first Medicare month (November) has a limit of zero. Correct. Caveat that could be added: the family figure assumes no spouse is also contributing toward the same family limit.

**K26. Income-related premium: 2026 uses 2024 MAGI. $202.90 up to $109,000 single / $218,000 joint; then $284.10, $405.80, $527.50, $649.20, $689.90.** STANDS
- https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/2026-medicare-parts-b-premiums-deductibles: the table shows exactly these totals, with the first tier "≤ $109,000 / ≤ $218,000 → $202.90" and the top tier "≥ $500,000 / ≥ $750,000 → $689.90".
- https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/20/418.1135
  > "we will use your modified adjusted gross income provided by IRS for the tax year 2 years prior to the effective year"

**K27. Whether the employer drug coverage is creditable varies by plan, so she must ask.** STANDS. K12 quote ("ask your plan – they have to tell you").
