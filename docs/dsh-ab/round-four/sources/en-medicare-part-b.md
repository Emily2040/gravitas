### 1. Part B late enrollment penalty does not apply with group health plan (GHP) coverage based on current employment, if she enrolls during the Special Enrollment Period (8 months after employment or coverage ends)
- Verdict: **confirmed**, with two precisions: (a) the SEP also lets her enroll in any month while she is still working and covered, not only in the 8 months afterwards; (b) the 8 months run from whichever ends first, the job or the coverage, starting the month after.
- URL: https://www.ssa.gov/help/iClaim_medSEP.html (date 2026-10-22)
  > "During any month you remain covered under the group health plan and your, or your spouse's, current employment continues; or
  > In the eight-month period that begins with the month after your group health plan coverage or the current employment it is based on ends, whichever comes first."
- URL: https://www.ssa.gov/medicare/plan/when-to-sign-up (date 2026-10-22)
  > "This is a "Special Enrollment Period," and there are no penalties if you sign up or add Part B during this time."
- URL: https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/medicare-basics/working-past-65 (date 2026-10-22; text inside the "available to everyone at the company" panel)

  > the period "ends with the last day of the eighth consecutive month in which the individual is at no time so enrolled."

### 2. "Employer 20+ employees" as a condition of that penalty exception

(Size matters for the SEP only for disabled people covered through a non-spouse family member, who need a "large group health plan".)

- URL: https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0600805270 (POMS HI 00805.270, date 2026-10-22)
  > "the individual must be covered under a group health plan (GHP) based on own or a spouse's current employment status in the first month of eligibility for SMI and for all months thereafter."
- URL: https://www.medicare.gov/health-drug-plans/coordination/who-pays-first (date 2026-10-22)

### 3. Initial Enrollment Period is 7 months
- Verdict: **confirmed**. Three months before the birthday month, the birthday month, three months after. If the birthday is on the 1st, the window shifts one month earlier.
- URL: https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/sign-up/when-does-medicare-coverage-start (date 2026-10-22)
  > "It lasts for 7 months, starting 3 months before you turn 65, and ending 3 months after the month you turn 65."
  > (Birthday on the 1st:) "It starts 4 months before you turn 65 and ends 2 months after the month you turn 65."
- Coverage start (same page): sign up before the birthday month, Part B starts the birthday month; sign up in the birthday month or the 3 months after, it starts the next month.
  > "Before the month you turn 65 | The month you turn 65" / "The month you turn 65, or during the 3 months after | The next month"
- Consequence for the asker: she turns 65 next month, so her IEP is already open this month.

## Facts for the key

**The premise and why it is wrong.** The coworker's rule ("sign up for Part B in your initial window or pay a penalty for life") is the general rule, but it does not apply to her. Because she is 65 and covered by a group health plan through her own current job, she qualifies for a Part B Special Enrollment Period. She can enroll in Part B in any month while she is still working and covered, or within 8 months after the job or the coverage ends (whichever comes first, counting from the month after), and pay no late penalty. So she does not need to enroll in Part B or start paying the premium this month. Employer size is not a condition of the SEP. It matters for who pays first: with about 400 employees (20 or more), her group plan pays first and Medicare would pay second. Paying for Part B now would buy secondary coverage, and it would also start her one-time Medigap open enrollment clock.

**What to do this month (a correct answer's list):**
1. Do not enroll in Part B yet (unless she prefers secondary coverage). Ask the benefits administrator to confirm that her coverage is an active-employee group plan (not COBRA or retiree) and that it stays primary after 65. By law, a 20+ employer must offer her the same benefits as younger workers.
2. Decide about premium-free Part A. It costs nothing (with about 10 years of Medicare-taxed work, hers or a spouse's), and if she signs up before her birthday month it starts the month she turns 65; with her employer plan primary, Medicare pays second. She should **not** take it if she or her employer is putting money into an HSA she wants to keep funding. Once she has any Medicare, her HSA contribution limit is zero. If she defers Part A and enrolls later, Part A reaches back up to 6 months (never before her 65th birthday month), so contributions must stop 6 months before she applies.
3. If she already receives Social Security, she will be enrolled in A and B automatically and must decline Part B using the card instructions. Starting Social Security at 65+ also brings automatic Part A, which ends HSA contributions.
4. Get the plan's creditable prescription drug coverage notice and keep it, so she can delay Part D without penalty.
5. Make sure the job will not end before her IEP closes (3 months after her birthday month). If the job or coverage ends inside the IEP, there is no SEP.
6. Later, when she retires: apply about a month before coverage ends, using CMS-40B (or the online SSA Part B application) plus CMS-L564 (Section A by her, Section B by the employer). COBRA does not extend the 8 months. She has 2 full months after the month employer coverage ends to join a Part D plan, and her 6-month Medigap open enrollment starts with Part B.

**Correct figures with arithmetic (2026, CMS fact sheet of 2025-11-14):**
- Standard Part B premium: $202.90/month (2025: $185.00; +$17.90). Per year: $202.90 x 12 = $2,434.80.
- If she enrolled in Part B this month (before her birthday month), coverage and premiums would start next month: November + December 2026 = 2 x $202.90 = $405.80 for 2026, then the 2027 premium (not yet announced as of 2026-10-22; do not quote a 2027 figure).
- Part B deductible: $283/year. Part A inpatient deductible: $1,736 per benefit period. Premium-Part A (only without enough work quarters): $311 or $565/month.
- Penalty mechanics, official 2026 example (no SEP, 24 months late): 20% x $202.90 = $40.58; $202.90 + $40.58 = $243.48, rounded to $243.50/month. Each full 12-month period adds 10%. With a valid SEP the penalty is $0.
- Part D penalty, if she ever goes 63+ days without creditable coverage: 1% x $38.99 = about $0.39 per uncovered month. Medicare.gov's 14-month example gives a 14% penalty; arithmetic (mine, using the page's rounding rule to the nearest $0.10): 14% x $38.99 = $5.4586, so $5.50/month added to the plan premium.

**Side claims an answer is likely to add, with correct values:**
- "You must have 20+ employees to delay Part B": imprecise. The SEP works with small or large employer plans; 20+ is the threshold for the group plan to pay first. With fewer than 20 employees Medicare pays first, and the employer plan may not pay without Part B (not her case).
- "Part A is retroactive 6 months": true for enrolling after 65, but never before the month she turned 65. In her IEP, Part A starts her birthday month (the month before if the birthday is on the 1st).
- HSA amounts if she takes Part A now (VARIES by plan type, birthday and full-year HSA eligibility): 2026 limit $4,400 self-only / $8,750 family, +$1,000 catch-up at 55+, prorated by months before Medicare. Example, birthday not on the 1st, Part A from November 1, 2026, 10 eligible months: self-only ($4,400 + $1,000) x 10/12 = $4,500; family ($8,750 + $1,000) x 10/12 = $8,125.
- Income-related premium (VARIES by income; 2026 uses 2024 MAGI): $202.90 up to $109,000 single / $218,000 joint; then $284.10, $405.80, $527.50, $649.20, $689.90 per month at the higher tiers.
- Whether her employer plan's drug coverage is creditable, and whether it coordinates in any special way with Medicare, VARIES by plan; she must ask the plan.
- Medigap prices and any extra state Medigap rights VARY by state (not checked here). The federal open enrollment is 6 months from the first month she is 65+ with Part B.
- Spouse: if she is married, a spouse's current-employment group coverage gives the same SEP; COBRA, retiree, VA, Marketplace/individual coverage and severance-based coverage never do.
- People with ESRD are not eligible for this SEP (POMS HI 00805.265); rarely relevant.
