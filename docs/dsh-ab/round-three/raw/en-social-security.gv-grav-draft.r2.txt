## First, two things in your plan don't work the way you described

**40 years of payments doesn't remove the early-claiming reduction.** Those 40 credits get you *eligible* — they're the entry ticket. The age reduction is separate and actuarial: with a full retirement age of 67, claiming at 62 pays **70% of your full benefit**, permanently. 40 credits changes nothing about that.

**Working while claiming does trigger a penalty** — the retirement earnings test. In 2027 it applies to anyone under FRA, no matter how long they've paid in. You lose **$1 of benefit for every $2 you earn above $25,560** ([SSA 2027 figures](https://www.ssa.gov/news/en/cola/factsheets/2027.html)). The test disappears only at 67, not before.

**And your first check won't be March.** You turn 62 on March 18, so you're not 62 for the whole of March. Your first month of entitlement is **April 2027**, paid in May ([POMS RS 00615.015](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0300615015)).

## The numbers

Your $2,400 is in today's dollars. The 3.6% COLA for January 2027 lifts it to about $2,486; 70% of that is **$1,740/month** ([reduction table](https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/retirement/planner/agereduction.html)).

## What the table doesn't include

- **Health insurance.** At 62 you're not on Medicare yet. If the part-time job doesn't cover you, this is likely your largest single line item — budget $500–900/month until 65.
- **State income tax**, if your state has one.
- **Federal tax is an estimate** assuming single filer, standard deduction, no other income. With $38,000 in wages, up to 85% of your benefits become taxable. File a [W-4V](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-w-4-v) so SSA withholds rather than facing a bill in April.

## Three things worth knowing before you file

**The withholding is lumpy, not smooth.** SSA withholds whole monthly checks. Starting in May 2027, expect roughly four checks to be swallowed entirely before the $6,220 is satisfied. Calendar 2027 will deliver about $9,400 from Social Security, not $14,660.

**It recurs.** As long as you earn $38,000, you'll lose that $6,220 every year until March 2032.

**The withheld money isn't entirely gone.** At 67, SSA recomputes your benefit to credit months when you received nothing (the "adjustment of the reduction factor"), recovering part of it — but not all, and not the 30% age reduction itself.

## The one lever you actually control

That $12,440 of earnings between $25,560 and $38,000 costs you $6,220 in benefits — a **50% effective marginal rate** on that slice. If your job would let you drop to about $25,500, you'd keep the full $20,880 and come out roughly even on cash while working 30% fewer hours. Worth pricing out before you claim.

And the tradeoff on timing: waiting to 67 pays about $2,486 with no earnings test at all — roughly 40% more per month, for life. That's a real cost of claiming now, though if you need the cash flow, that's a legitimate reason.
