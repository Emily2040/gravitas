## What's actually true

**Your 40 years don't protect you from the early-claiming reduction.** That reduction depends only on your age when you claim. Your full retirement age is 67 (born 1965), and claiming at 62 takes 30% off permanently: $2,400 becomes $1,680. Forty years of contributions make you *eligible* — you need ten — and they raise your base amount. They don't change the age formula.

**Working while claiming does trigger a penalty.** That's the retirement earnings test. In 2027, if you're under 67, SSA withholds $1 of benefits for every $2 you earn above **$25,560** ([2027 COLA release, quoting SSA](https://adasoutheast.org/cost-of-living-adjustment-cola-information-for-2027/)). At $38,000 in gross wages you're $12,440 over, so **$6,220 gets withheld**.

**You can't start in March.** You turn 62 on March 18, 2027, but SSA requires you to be 62 for the *entire* month. Your first month of entitlement is April 2027.

The arithmetic: $2,400 × 70% = $1,680; × 1.036 for the January COLA = **$1,740**. Nine months = **$15,660**; minus $6,220 = **$9,440 for the year** — not the ~$21,600 that nine months of $2,400 would have come to.

## Should you still claim at 62?

Probably not, unless you need the cash now.

- **Claim at 62:** ~$9,400 in 2027, and a reduced check for life. One break: months the earnings test wipes out get credited back at 67, so your permanent cut ends up smaller than 30% — but you still collect less per month than if you'd waited.
- **Wait to 67:** nothing until 2032, then the full $2,400 plus five years of COLAs, no withholding, no earnings test, for life.

On my arithmetic, waiting pulls ahead around age 79–80. Life expectancy at 62 is mid-80s, so waiting usually wins — if you can cover 2027–2031 on the paycheck alone.

If you do claim, budget ~$950/month average from Social Security in 2027, not $2,400. And you can file about four months ahead, so the window for an April start opens around December 2026.

## Three things to check before you decide

1. **Your exact age-62 amount** in your my Social Security account — the $1,740 is derived from your $2,400, and the statement may be a year or two old.
2. **Filing status and state** — I assumed single, standard deduction, no state income tax. State tax and any 401(k) or health premiums lower the take-home line.
3. **Health coverage through 65** — Medicare starts March 2030, so no Part B premium comes out in 2027, but you need a plan for the gap if you leave the job.

One more: with $38,000 in wages, up to 85% of your benefits becomes taxable, which is why that federal line is higher than what you're paying now.
