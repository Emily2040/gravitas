## First, two things in your plan don't work the way you described

**40 years of payments doesn't remove the early-claiming reduction.** Those 40 credits get you *eligible* — they're the entry ticket. The age reduction is separate and actuarial: with a full retirement age of 67, claiming at 62 pays **70% of your full benefit**, permanently. The formula is 5/9 of 1% for each of the first 36 months early, then 5/12 of 1% for each month beyond — 60 months gives exactly 168/240 ([POMS RS 00615.101](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0300615101)). 40 credits changes nothing about that.

**Working while claiming does trigger a penalty** — the retirement earnings test. In 2027 it applies to anyone under FRA, no matter how long they've paid in. You lose **$1 of benefit for every $2 you earn above $25,560** ([SSA 2027 figures](https://www.ssa.gov/news/en/cola/factsheets/2027.html)). The test disappears only at 67, not before.

**And your first check won't be March.** You turn 62 on March 18, so you're not 62 for the whole of March. Your first month of entitlement is **April 2027**, paid in May ([POMS RS 00615.015](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0300615015)).

## What the table doesn't include

- **Health insurance.** At 62 you're not on Medicare yet. If the part-time job doesn't cover you, price a Marketplace plan — this is often the largest single line item in the budget.
- **State income tax**, if your state has one.
- **Federal tax is an estimate** using 2026 brackets: standard deduction $16,100, 10% to $12,400, 12% to $50,400, [per the IRS](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-releases-tax-inflation-adjustments-for-tax-year-2026-including-amendments-from-the-one-big-beautiful-bill). It assumes single, standard deduction, no other income. With $38,000 in wages, 85% of your benefits become taxable. File a [W-4V](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-w-4-v) so SSA withholds rather than facing a bill in April.

## Three things worth knowing before you file

**The withholding is lumpy, not smooth.** SSA withholds whole monthly checks. You'll get eight checks in calendar 2027, and roughly three and a half of them get swallowed — so expect about **$7,700** from Social Security that year, not $14,660.

**It recurs.** As long as you earn $38,000, you'll lose that $6,220 every year until March 2032.

**The withheld money isn't entirely gone.** At 67, SSA recomputes your benefit to credit months when you received nothing, recovering part of the withholding — but not the 30% age reduction itself.

## The one lever you actually control

Every dollar between $25,560 and $38,000 buys you 50 cents of benefit. Dropping your hours to $25,560 keeps the full $20,880 but gives up $12,440 in wages — a net loss of $6,220. So it isn't free money; it's worth doing only if those hours are worth less to you than $6,220.

Waiting to 67 would pay $2,486 with no earnings test at all — about 40% more per month, for life. That's the real cost of claiming now, though needing the cash flow is a legitimate reason.
