Before you send anything — the agent had that backwards.

Under the DOT refund rule ([14 CFR part 260](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/part-260), in effect since late 2024), a flight cancelled **for any reason, including weather**, entitles you to a cash refund to your original payment method if you don't take the rebooking ([summary of the rule](https://komonews.com/news/consumer/new-rule-will-soon-require-airlines-to-provide-automatic-refunds-when-flights-are-canceled-department-of-transportation-rules-immediate-transportation-secretary-pete-buttigieg-flight-changes-domestic-international-dot)). The weather carve-out covers things like hotels and meals, not your fare. So the $412 is very likely refundable in cash, and accepting a credit is generally treated as choosing the alternative — which ends the refund right.

Your call. If you still want the credit, here's the message:

```
Subject: Travel credit — cancelled UA flight, ORD–DEN, [date]

Hello,

My Chicago O'Hare–Denver flight on [date] (confirmation [code]) was cancelled
because of thunderstorms, and the rebooking offered was for the next afternoon.
I did not travel. I'd like to accept a travel credit for the $412 fare.

Please confirm in writing the credit amount, that it is valid for at least 12
months from today's date, and the expiration date and any restrictions that apply.

Thank you,
[Name]
[Phone / email]
[MileagePlus number, if you have one]
```

I worded the expiry as "12 months from today's date" on purpose: United's credits usually run about a year from the **original ticket date**, not the cancellation date, so a plain "one year" can come up short.
