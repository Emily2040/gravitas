## First — that's probably not a valid denial

Before you send anything: don't authorize the $3,400 yet.

An oil change at Jiffy Lube does not void a Honda powertrain warranty. Two reasons:

**The law.** Under the Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act, Honda may not condition its warranty on you using Honda-branded or dealer-provided service ([15 U.S.C. § 2302(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/2302)). A manufacturer can deny a *specific* claim only if it proves the failure was caused by improper service or a wrong part — not because you went elsewhere.

**Honda's own words.** Honda's owner's manuals state plainly: *"service at a dealer is not mandatory to keep your warranties in effect. Maintenance may be done by any qualified service facility or person"* ([Honda owner's manual](https://www.haccord.org/important_maintenance_precautions-569.html)).

And the logic doesn't hold regardless: engine oil has no path to a slipping transmission. At 41,000 miles you're squarely inside 5 years/60,000 miles, which covers the transmission and transaxle.

Three things worth doing before you pay:

- **Get the denial in writing**, with the specific warranty-booklet provision cited. A verbal "not covered" from a service writer isn't a Honda determination — the dealer is a franchise, not Honda.
- **Call American Honda Customer Service: 1-800-999-1009** ([Honda contact info](https://techinfo.honda.com/rjanisis/pubs/om/ah/a3v02323iom/enu/details/info-CustomerServiceInformation.html)). Ask them to review the denial. Dealers are reimbursed for warranty work at Honda's rates, which gives them a reason to call it customer-pay.
- **Check one thing yourself:** did Jiffy Lube ever service the *transmission fluid*, not just the oil? Wrong CVT fluid genuinely can cause slipping and would be a real defense. If it was oil changes only, the dealer has nothing.

Once you pay, recovering the money is much harder. I'm not a lawyer, but this is a well-settled area and $3,400 is worth a phone call.

## The email

I wrote it to do what you asked — commit to the repair, ask for price relief — but I did **not** write "I'll go ahead," and I added one question. Details below the draft.

```
Subject: 2023 CR-V — transmission repair and pricing

Dear [Mr./Ms. Last name],

Thank you for diagnosing the transmission on my 2023 CR-V. I want to
get the car back on the road, so I'm ready to move forward with the
repair.

Before I authorize the $3,400, may I ask two things?

First, is there any flexibility on the price? If Honda can share in the
cost, or if you're able to adjust the labor or parts, I'd be grateful.
I'd also like to keep servicing the car with your department.

Second, could you send me the warranty denial in writing, citing the
specific provision in the Honda warranty booklet it rests on? My
understanding is that routine maintenance at an independent shop does
not by itself void the powertrain warranty. I'm not trying to make this
difficult — I just need it for my records before I commit to this
amount.

I'd appreciate hearing back this week. Thank you for your help.

[Name]
[Phone]
VIN: [__________]
```

**What I changed, and why:**

- I softened your commitment from authorizing the work to being ready to proceed. Same intent, but a written authorization before the warranty question is answered weakens your position later.
- Added the written-denial request. It's framed as a records request, not an accusation, so it stays polite — but it forces the dealer to either produce a provision or back off.
- Removed any version of "that's on me." Never put an admission of fault in writing to the party deciding whether to charge you.

**If you'd rather skip the warranty angle entirely:** delete the second question and change the first sentence to *"I'll go ahead with the repair."* That's your call — but I'd make the phone call first, since it costs nothing and could save you the full amount.

One last thing: dig up your Jiffy Lube receipts. If the dealer does push on maintenance, you'll want to show the oil changes hit Honda's intervals.
