Two assumptions in your plan don't hold, and together they cut your Social Security income by more than a third. Here's the corrected math, then the budget.

## Claiming at 62 is a permanent 30% cut

Forty years of contributions makes you insured — you need only 40 credits, about 10 years of work. It does not exempt you from the early-claiming reduction. That reduction depends only on how many months before full retirement age you start.

Your full retirement age is 67, because you were born after 1959. Claiming at 62 is 60 months early: 36 months at 5/9 of 1%, plus 24 months at 5/12 of 1%, equals a 30% reduction ([SSA](https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/retirement/planner/agereduction.html)).

| | |
|---|---|
| Your stated full benefit at 67 | $2,400 |
| × 70% (age-62 factor) | $1,680 in today's dollars |
| × 1.036 (2027 COLA) | **≈ $1,740 in 2027 dollars** |

Also, "40 years paid in" doesn't raise your benefit beyond your 35 highest-earning years. Years 36–40 only help if they replace a lower year already in that average.

## Working while claiming triggers the earnings test

The 2027 limit for someone under full retirement age is **$25,560** a year, up from $24,480 in 2026 ([SSA 2027 COLA fact sheet](https://www.ssa.gov/news/en/cola/factsheets/2027.html)). Above that, SSA withholds $1 of benefits for every $2 you earn.

- $38,000 − $25,560 = $12,440 over the limit
- $12,440 ÷ 2 = **$6,220 withheld for the year**, about $518 a month

SSA withholds whole checks, not partial ones. That's roughly 3.6 checks, so you'll see about eight payments a year instead of twelve.

The withheld money isn't lost forever. At 67, SSA recalculates your benefit to credit the months your checks were fully withheld, which raises your monthly amount somewhat. The 30% early-claiming cut is *not* restored that way.

## Monthly budget, 2027

Annualized run-rate, so you can see the shape of a normal year:

| Line | Monthly |
|---|---|
| Part-time wages (gross) | $3,167 |
| Social Security, before withholding | $1,740 |
| **Total gross** | **$4,907** |
| Earnings-test withholding | −$518 |
| FICA (7.65% of wages) | −$242 |
| Federal income tax (estimate) | −$320 |
| **Net** | **≈ $3,827** |

That's roughly $58,900 gross and $45,900 net for a full year.

## What's behind those deductions

**Federal tax.** With $38,000 of wages, about 85% of your benefits become taxable, which is why this line is larger than you'd expect. I ran it as a single filer with the standard deduction. If you're married filing jointly, the thresholds are much wider and this line drops substantially — possibly to near zero.

**FICA.** If you're a W-2 employee, 7.65% comes out of your check automatically. If you're self-employed, it's 15.3%, and that line roughly doubles.

**Not included:** state income tax, health insurance, and Medicare. Part B doesn't start until you turn 65 in 2030, so there's no premium in 2027.

## 2027 won't look like this month to month

Your first month of eligibility is **April 2027**, not March. Social Security requires you to be 62 for the entire month, and since you were born on the 18th, you aren't 62 for all of March ([POMS RS 00615.015](https://secure.ssa.gov/apps10/poms.nsf/lnx/0300615015)). Your first payment would arrive in May.

Then SSA holds back checks to cover the earnings test. Expect roughly four actual deposits in calendar 2027 — about $7,000 total — with a steadier pattern in 2028.

## One thing worth pausing on before you file

The usual argument for claiming early is "you collect more checks." The earnings test takes most of those checks away, so you'd absorb the full permanent 30% reduction without getting the early money in exchange. That's the worst of both.

If you waited until you stopped working, or until 67, you'd lock in roughly $2,486 a month (in 2027 dollars) with no withholding — about $746 more per month, every month, for life, plus larger COLAs because they compound on a bigger base.

You can undo a claim within 12 months using [Form SSA-521](https://www.ssa.gov/forms/ssa-521.html), but you have to repay everything you received, so it's a narrow escape hatch.
