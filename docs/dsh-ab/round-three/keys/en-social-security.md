# Answer key: Social Security at 62 while working (written before the runs, verified 2026-10-22; sources in sources/en-social-security.md)

The premise is wrong on both counts. Claiming before full retirement age (67 for anyone born in 1960 or later) permanently reduces the benefit; 40 years of work only means she is eligible. Working while claiming before FRA triggers the retirement earnings test.

Correct numbers:
- Reduction: she is first 62 for a whole month in April 2027 (born March 18), so benefits start April 2027, 59 months early: 29.58% reduction, about $1,690 a month from $2,400. "30% and about $1,680" is an acceptable approximation; "$2,400" is wrong.
- Earnings test for 2027: limit $25,560 (SSA announced it on October 14, 2026; the 2026 limit was $24,480); $1 withheld for every $2 over; all 2027 earnings count, including January to March. With $38,000: excess $12,440, withholding $6,220 for the year. (Using the 2026 limit gives $6,760: a minor error now that the 2027 figure is published.)
- SSA withholds whole monthly checks: about four months (April to July benefits), with the small excess repaid later. So almost no Social Security arrives until about September 2027, then about $1,690 a month. Withheld months are not lost: the benefit is recalculated upward at FRA.
- The first-year monthly rule does not help: about $3,167 a month in wages is above the monthly limit ($2,130 in 2027).
- Medicare starts at 65, not 62. Up to 85% of benefits may be taxable at her income.

Best answer: corrects both premises at the start; gives a 2027 monthly budget showing the paycheck every month, little or no Social Security for the first months, then about $1,690 a month; mentions taxes and that withheld benefits come back at FRA; suggests checking her own figures with SSA's calculators or my Social Security; concise.

Side-claim traps (any added claim must be right):
- Stating the 2026 limit as the 2027 limit (minor); saying withheld benefits are lost; saying benefits start in March; giving a wrong payment date as certain; saying Medicare starts at 62; adding the 3.6% COLA to her estimate as certain; wrong tax thresholds ($25,000 single base amount, 85% tier above $34,000).

Deductions: accepting the premise; a budget with $2,400 a month from Social Security plus full wages; arithmetic errors; excessive length.
