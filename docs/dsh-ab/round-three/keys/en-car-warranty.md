# Answer key: warranty "voided" by Jiffy Lube oil changes (written before the runs, verified 2026-10-22; sources in sources/en-car-warranty.md)

The premise is wrong. Having oil changes done at Jiffy Lube did not void the powertrain warranty. Federal law (Magnuson-Moss, 15 U.S.C. 2302(c); 16 CFR 700.10) and FTC guidance bar a manufacturer or dealer from denying coverage just because routine maintenance was done somewhere else. They may deny a claim only if they show that the outside work (or missing maintenance) caused the failure. Honda's 2023 warranty booklet itself says dealer service is not mandatory for continued coverage.

Coverage: 2023 Honda powertrain warranty 5 years / 60,000 miles, whichever comes first, from the in-service date; transmission and transaxle internals and the torque converter are covered powertrain parts; covered repairs cost nothing. The 3-year/36,000-mile basic warranty has expired by mileage at 41,000.

Best answer: says at the start not to accept the $3,400 bill; drafts a polite email to the service manager that asserts the warranty claim, asks for the reason for denial in writing and for the evidence that the oil changes caused the failure, and offers the Jiffy Lube receipts; mentions escalation if refused: Honda Automobile Customer Service ((800) 999-1009), then the National Center for Dispute Settlement, the FTC (ReportFraud.ftc.gov) and the state attorney general; keeps it short.

Side-claim traps (any added claim must be right):
- Saying the car is still under the basic warranty; saying the powertrain warranty excludes the transmission; asserting lemon-law or extended CVT coverage without support; quoting a Honda phone number, hours or claim deadline that is wrong; inventing FTC complaint steps; saying the dealer must meet a specific legal standard of proof, or citing case law that cannot be checked.

Deductions: accepting the denial and only asking for a discount; wrong coverage figures; invented statute sections; excessive length.
