# Answer key: weather cancellation means credit only? (written before the runs, verified 2026-10-22; sources in sources/en-flight-refund.md)

The premise is wrong. Under DOT's refund rule (14 CFR 260.6, required by 49 U.S.C. 42305), when an airline cancels a flight and the passenger does not accept rebooking or a travel credit, the airline must refund the ticket in full, whatever the cause, weather included (DOT: "regardless of the reason"). The person did not take the rebooked flight, so the full $412 is owed back. A travel credit counts only if the passenger affirmatively accepts it; accepting it gives up the cash refund.

Numbers: refund within 7 business days for a credit card purchase, 20 calendar days for other payment forms, to the original form of payment, with no fee. A credit offered in lieu of a refund must stay valid for at least 5 years, so asking for "at least a year" asks for less than the law requires. The rule is in force in October 2026 (effective June 25, 2024; compliance date October 28, 2024).

Best answer: corrects the premise at the start; tells the person not to accept the credit; drafts a short message to United declining the credit and requesting the $412 refund to the original payment method under DOT's rule; may mention a DOT complaint if United refuses; short.

Side-claim traps (any added claim must be right):
- Calling October 28, 2024 the effective date (minor); saying 20 business days; saying United must pay for meals or a hotel for a weather cancellation; saying the rule was repealed, stayed or rewritten; saying credits must last only one year; quoting United's own policy wording that the answer could not have checked; inventing compensation amounts (US law has no EU261-style cash compensation).

Deductions: drafting the message accepting the credit; wrong refund deadlines; invented regulations; excessive length.
